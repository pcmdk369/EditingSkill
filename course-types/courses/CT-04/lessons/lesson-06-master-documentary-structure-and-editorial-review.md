# CT-04 Lesson 06 — Master Documentary Structure and Editorial Review

- Course: CT-04 — Documentary & Interview Editing
- Level: Master
- Estimated time: 120–180 minutes
- Prerequisites: Previous CT-04 lessons and access to authorized documentary/interview source material
- Tools/environment: Any NLE, transcript/logging tool and secure project storage; record OS, application and version
- Version/review date: Tool-neutral; verify legal, platform and software requirements for the production jurisdiction and delivery destination

## Learning outcomes
Direct a complete short documentary; balance competing perspectives; manage editorial review and picture lock with traceable decisions.

## Key definitions and terminology
- **Documentary evidence:** Source material used to support, qualify or challenge a factual account.
- **Attribution:** Clear identification of who made a statement or where information originated.
- **Select:** A logged source passage considered for the edit.
- **Paper edit:** Written sequence of quotes and scenes before timeline assembly.
- **B-roll:** Supporting footage used to add context or visual information.
- **J-cut / L-cut:** Audio that begins before or continues after its corresponding picture cut.
- **Provenance:** Record of origin, ownership and transformations of an asset.
- **Editorial integrity:** Faithful representation of source meaning and evidential limits.

## Purpose and professional relevance
Master-level documentary editing requires structural judgment and accountable editorial governance. A coherent arc may include contradiction, ambiguity or an unresolved ending. The editor should not force neat resolution where evidence does not support it. Review is a structured process that protects accuracy, ethics and craft.

## Tools, requirements and alternatives
Use a spreadsheet or database for source logs, a secure transcript workflow, a non-linear editor and backed-up storage. Exact menu paths are application/version-specific. Use only learner-created, licensed or otherwise authorized media. For sensitive interviews, use approved storage and processing; do not upload material to an external AI service without permission and an acceptable privacy basis.

## Procedure
1. Write a one-sentence thesis and a separate open question. 2. Build a scene/sequence map showing evidence and perspective. 3. Check voice balance and missing stakeholders. 4. Test the rough cut with viewers unfamiliar with the subject. 5. Record confusion and unsupported inference, not only preference. 6. Run factual, legal/rights, subject-sensitivity and accessibility reviews. 7. Maintain a decision log for contested edits. 8. Establish picture-lock criteria and a controlled change process.

## Worked example
A film contains two opposing accounts. The structure presents each fairly, shows what can be independently verified and clearly marks what remains disputed rather than inventing a compromise.

## Dry run / annotated walkthrough
Reviewer says the ending is “too uncertain.” Check whether uncertainty reflects the evidence. Do not add an unsupported resolution merely to create emotional closure.

## Guided practice
Create a 3-minute rough cut from interview and B-roll; conduct two viewer reviews and one factual review; revise with a change log.

## Independent assignment
Create a 3-minute rough cut from interview and B-roll; conduct two viewer reviews and one factual review; revise with a change log. Extend the task into a documented mini-sequence and submit source references, decisions and review evidence.

## Common errors and recovery
| Symptom | Likely cause | Diagnosis | Recovery |
|---|---|---|---|
| One perspective dominates without editorial explanation | Source imbalance or selective assembly | Compare screen time, claims and evidence across sources | Add context or explain the evidential limitation |
| Ending implies a resolution not supported by sources | Narrative closure prioritized over evidence | Trace final statement to source and claim ledger | Retain uncertainty or revise the conclusion |
| Picture lock is repeatedly broken | No approval/change control | Audit version history and review decisions | Define lock criteria and controlled change requests |
| Reviewer preference is treated as factual correction | Review roles unclear | Separate factual, legal, accessibility and creative notes | Route each issue to the correct approval owner |

## Knowledge check
1. Why must the editor distinguish testimony from independently verified fact?
2. What source information should accompany an important quote or visual?
3. What is one way an edit can unintentionally misrepresent a participant?
4. Which human review gate is essential before delivery?

## Answer key / instructor guidance
1. Testimony establishes what a participant reports; external claims may require corroboration.
2. Source identity, timecode, origin/date, rights and context.
3. Removing qualification, changing chronology, misattributing speech or using misleading B-roll.
4. Factual, consent/rights, accessibility and technical reviews appropriate to the brief.

## Assessment rubric — 20 points
| Criterion | Points | Evidence |
|---|---:|---|
| Source accuracy and context | 5 | Source log/timecodes and contextual review |
| Editorial reasoning | 5 | Story/sequence choices and explanation |
| Rights, consent and ethics | 4 | Risk/rights record |
| Reproducible workflow | 3 | Project organization and steps |
| QC and reflection | 3 | Review evidence and corrections |

Pass: 16/20. Misattributed quotes, fabricated evidence or unauthorized use of restricted material are mandatory remediation items.

## Quality-control checklist
- [ ] Source and timecode can be traced.
- [ ] Quote remains faithful to full context.
- [ ] Facts, opinions and allegations are distinguished.
- [ ] Rights, releases and consent are documented.
- [ ] Sensitive information is handled appropriately.
- [ ] Review findings and corrections are recorded.
