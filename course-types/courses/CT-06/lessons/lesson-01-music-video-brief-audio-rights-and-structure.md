# CT-06 Lesson 01 — Music Video Brief, Audio Rights and Structure

- Course: CT-06 — Music Video & Performance Editing
- Level: Foundation / Beginner
- Estimated time: 120–180 minutes
- Prerequisites: Basic video editing and music literacy
- Tools/environment: Any NLE with waveform, multicamera, retiming and graphics capabilities; record OS/app/version
- Version/review date: Tool-neutral; rights and platform requirements vary by territory

## Learning outcomes
Read a music-video brief, identify the master audio, map song sections and verify music/performer/footage rights before editing.

## Key definitions
- **Beat:** A recurring pulse in music.
- **Bar/measure:** A grouped set of beats.
- **Phrase:** A musical unit often perceived as a complete idea.
- **Transient:** A short, rapid change in sound energy.
- **Sync:** Alignment of picture and audio events.
- **Match cut:** A cut linking shots through visual or action similarity.
- **Speed ramp:** A change in playback speed over time.
- **Visual motif:** A recurring visual element that supports the concept.

## Purpose and theory
Music videos are structured around music, performance and visual interpretation. Beat, bar and phrase are different musical units. The editor should understand the track's form and the intended relationship between lyrics, performance and imagery. Rights to a recording, composition, performer and footage may be separate.

## Tools, requirements and alternatives
Use a current NLE with audio waveform display and the required edit functions. For audio analysis, use a trusted media tool where necessary. Record application/version. Use original or properly licensed recordings, compositions, footage, artwork and lyrics. This course does not grant music or likeness rights.

## Procedure
1. Confirm creative brief, audience and deliverables. 2. Identify the exact approved audio master and version. 3. Verify licenses/permissions for composition, recording, performance and footage. 4. Listen to the complete track and mark sections. 5. Identify bars, phrases, tempo changes and breaks. 6. Build a visual motif and shot plan. 7. Record sync and export specifications. 8. Preserve source audio and original media.

## Worked example
A 90-second performance cut uses a licensed recording. The editor marks intro, verse, chorus, bridge and ending, then maps visual energy to those sections rather than cutting randomly.

## Dry run
Two audio files have similar names but different edits. Compare duration, waveform and approval note; never sync to an unapproved version.

## Guided practice
Create a music-video brief, rights register and song-section map for a 60–90 second edit.

## Independent assignment
Extend the guided task into a complete short performance edit. Submit project, review export, rights/source manifest and a written explanation of editorial choices.

## Common errors and recovery

|---|---|---|---|

## Knowledge check
1. How do beat, bar and phrase differ?
2. Why is waveform inspection not enough to understand musical structure?
3. What can make a match cut effective?
4. Why should speed changes be reviewed for meaning and artifacts?
5. Which rights may be separate in a music-video production?

## Answer guidance
1. Beat is pulse, bar groups beats, phrase is a larger musical idea.
2. Waveform peaks show energy changes, not necessarily beat/phrase or musical meaning.
3. A meaningful similarity in movement, framing, shape or action.
4. They can distort performance timing and create interpolation artifacts.
5. Composition, recording, performance, footage, artwork and lyrics.
