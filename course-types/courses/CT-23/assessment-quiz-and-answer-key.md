# CT-23 — Subtitles, Captions & Localization Assessment

**Total:** 50 points | **Suggested time:** 45–60 minutes

## Part A — Multiple choice (10 × 2 = 20)

1. A cue is: A. Timed text event B. Video codec C. Camera shot D. License.
2. Reading speed relates: A. Text amount to display time B. Frame size C. Audio peak D. File name.
3. Captions may include: A. Meaningful non-speech sound B. Only titles C. Only hashtags D. No speaker info.
4. A transcript should be: A. Verified against audio B. Guessed C. Published without review D. Replaced by AI.
5. SRT and WebVTT have: A. Different syntax conventions B. Identical structure always C. No timestamps D. No encoding.
6. Localization considers: A. Locale/culture/idiom B. Only word substitution C. File size D. Camera angle.
7. UTF-8 relates to: A. Text encoding B. Frame rate C. Loudness D. Color.
8. Machine translation should be: A. Human-reviewed B. Automatically certified C. Published without review D. Used as legal authority.
9. Subtitle timing should be checked against: A. Exact final master B. Any source C. Filename only D. Thumbnail.
10. A professional handoff includes: A. Locale/version manifest B. Only screenshot C. No glossary D. No QC.

## Part B — Short answer (5 × 3 = 15)

11. Distinguish captions and subtitles in common usage.
12. Name three factors affecting cue segmentation.
13. Why must negation and numbers receive careful review?
14. What does a subtitle style guide define?
15. What should a multilingual delivery manifest record?

## Part C — Applied case (15)

16. A translation is 40% longer than source and cue timing is too short. Explain a responsible adaptation. (8)
17. An SRT displays garbled accented characters and is timed to an older edit. Explain correction. (7)

## Answer key
1 A; 2 A; 3 A; 4 A; 5 A; 6 A; 7 A; 8 A; 9 A; 10 A.
11. Captions represent speech and relevant sound; subtitles typically represent dialogue, often translation; conventions vary. (3)
12. Syntax, reading speed, cue duration, language and shot changes. (3)
13. They can reverse or materially change meaning. (3)
14. Punctuation, line breaks, speaker labels, sound cues and timing conventions. (3)
15. Master ID, language/locale, format, encoding, version, reviewer and approval. (3)
16. Re-segment at natural phrases, condense only without loss of meaning, extend duration where possible, adjust edit if allowed and obtain fluent review. (8)
17. Export UTF-8, validate syntax, retime to exact final master, test in target player and update manifest. (7)

## Performance bands
45–50 exemplary; 40–44 competent; 35–39 developing; below 35 remediation. Translation meaning and source-version accuracy are mandatory.
