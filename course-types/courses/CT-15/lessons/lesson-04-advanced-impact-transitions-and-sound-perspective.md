# CT-15 Lesson 04 — Impact, Transitions and Sound Perspective

- Course: CT-15 — Sound Design & Foley
- Level: Advanced
- Estimated time: 120–180 minutes
- Prerequisites: Previous CT-15 lessons and authorized audio/video source material
- Tools/environment: DAW/audio editor, microphone/recorder and sound library; record OS/app/version and audio format
- Version/review date: Tool-neutral; verify rights and delivery specifications

## Learning outcomes
Design impacts, whooshes, transitions and spatial perspective that reinforce narrative while avoiding fatigue and misleading physical cues.

## Key definitions
- **Foley:** Performed sound recorded to match picture.
- **Ambience:** Background sound establishing environment and perspective.
- **Transient:** Short, rapid onset of a sound.
- **Decay:** How a sound's level reduces after its onset.
- **Perspective:** Perceived distance/position based on level, frequency, reverberation and stereo cues.
- **Stem:** Submix export of related tracks aligned to common timeline.
- **Sonic identity:** Recurring sound characteristics associated with a project or brand.
- **Provenance:** Record of source and transformation history.

## Purpose and theory
Designed effects can emphasize movement and scene transitions. Impacts require timing, transient shape and decay. Reverb and filtering communicate space. Excessive sub-bass, loudness or repeated impacts can fatigue listeners and reduce clarity. Design should follow picture and story intent.

## Tools, requirements and alternatives
Use a current DAW/audio editor, suitable recording equipment and authorized sound assets. Record sample rate, channel layout, app/version and license. Preserve raw recordings and maintain separate processed copies.

## Procedure
1. Identify visual motion and narrative emphasis. 2. Choose source layers with distinct roles. 3. Align transient to action. 4. Shape attack/decay and frequency balance. 5. Add space/reverb consistent with location. 6. Check stereo placement and mono compatibility. 7. Avoid masking dialogue or key effects. 8. Review on multiple systems.

## Worked example
A door slam uses a close transient, body resonance and room tail appropriate to the space.

## Dry run
A cinematic impact implies an explosion that did not occur. Use a restrained transition or label stylized sound where context requires.

## Guided practice
Design three transitions and two impacts for a short montage.

## Independent assignment
Build a second scene or sound library using the same workflow. Submit raw source, editable session, processed assets, rights log and QC evidence.

## Common errors and recovery
| Symptom | Likely cause | Diagnosis | Recovery |
|---|---|---|---|
| Submit effect layers, processing notes and scene playback. | Sound-design issue | Inspect source, perspective and mix | Correct and audition |

## Knowledge check
1. What is the difference between Foley and production sound?
2. How do level, EQ and reverb communicate perspective?
3. Why should loops be checked for repetition?
4. What belongs in a sound-library rights record?
5. Why must stems share a common start time?

## Answer guidance
1. Foley is performed to picture; production sound is captured during original recording.
2. Distant sounds are often quieter, filtered and more reverberant depending on environment.
3. Repetition can reveal the loop and break realism.
4. Source, creator, license, restrictions and technical metadata.
5. It allows accurate recombination and synchronization.
