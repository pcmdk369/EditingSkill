# CT-15 Lesson 01 — Sound Design Fundamentals, Listening and Session Organization

- Course: CT-15 — Sound Design & Foley
- Level: Foundation / Beginner
- Estimated time: 120–180 minutes
- Prerequisites: Basic audio editing and listening skills
- Tools/environment: DAW/audio editor, microphone/recorder and sound library; record OS/app/version and audio format
- Version/review date: Tool-neutral; verify rights and delivery specifications

## Learning outcomes
Understand sound-design roles, listening perspective, source categories and organized sessions for effects, ambience and Foley.

## Key definitions
- **Foley:** Performed sound recorded to match picture.
- **Ambience:** Background sound establishing environment and perspective.
- **Transient:** Short, rapid onset of a sound.
- **Decay:** How a sound's level reduces after its onset.
- **Perspective:** Perceived distance/position based on level, frequency, reverberation and stereo cues.
- **Stem:** Submix export of related tracks aligned to common timeline.
- **Sonic identity:** Recurring sound characteristics associated with a project or brand.
- **Provenance:** Record of source and transformation history.

## Purpose and theory
Sound design creates or shapes the sonic environment of a story. It may use production sound, recorded effects, Foley, ambience, designed elements and silence. A sound should support perspective and action without confusing the audience or misrepresenting reality. Organized tracks and metadata make complex sessions manageable.

## Tools, requirements and alternatives
Use a current DAW/audio editor, suitable recording equipment and authorized sound assets. Record sample rate, channel layout, app/version and license. Preserve raw recordings and maintain separate processed copies.

## Procedure
1. Read the scene and identify emotional/physical sound needs. 2. Separate dialogue, production sound, ambience, Foley, designed effects and music. 3. Inventory authorized sound assets. 4. Label tracks and categories. 5. Set session sample rate and channel layout from brief. 6. Listen to the scene without added sound. 7. Build a sound spotting list. 8. Save source and session backups.

## Worked example
A short hallway scene needs footsteps, room tone, door movement and distant city ambience. The editor records what is already present before adding anything.

## Dry run
A sound library file has unclear license. Do not use it until rights are confirmed or replace it with an authorized recording.

## Guided practice
Create a spotting list and organized sound-design session for a 30-second scene.

## Independent assignment
Build a second scene or sound library using the same workflow. Submit raw source, editable session, processed assets, rights log and QC evidence.

## Common errors and recovery
| Symptom | Likely cause | Diagnosis | Recovery |
|---|---|---|---|
| Submit session structure, source log and spotting list. | Sound-design issue | Inspect source, perspective and mix | Correct and audition |

## Knowledge check
1. What is the difference between Foley and production sound?
2. How do level, EQ and reverb communicate perspective?
3. Why should loops be checked for repetition?
4. What belongs in a sound-library rights record?
5. Why must stems share a common start time?

## Answer guidance
1. Foley is performed to picture; production sound is captured during original recording.
2. Distant sounds are often quieter, filtered and more reverberant depending on environment.
3. Repetition can reveal the loop and break realism.
4. Source, creator, license, restrictions and technical metadata.
5. It allows accurate recombination and synchronization.

## Quality-control checklist
- [ ] Sound cues support story and picture.
- [ ] Foley sync and performance are convincing.
- [ ] Ambience perspective and loops are checked.
- [ ] Mix does not mask dialogue.
- [ ] Source licenses and permissions recorded.
- [ ] AI/synthetic material reviewed and disclosed where required.
- [ ] Stems align and package is reproducible.
