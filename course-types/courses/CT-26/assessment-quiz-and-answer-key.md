# CT-26 — AI Voice, Dubbing & Speech Workflows Assessment

**Total:** 50 points | **Suggested time:** 45–60 minutes

## Part A — Multiple choice (10 × 2 = 20)
1. Speaker diarization proves a speaker's legal identity. A. True B. False
2. An uncertain transcript word should be: A. Guessed B. Flagged and checked C. Deleted silently D. Replaced by AI
3. Localization includes: A. Only literal translation B. Locale, terminology and cultural adaptation C. Export bitrate D. Noise reduction
4. Dubbing quality includes: A. Timing and performance B. Only word count C. Only loudness D. File name
5. A public voice sample grants cloning permission. A. True B. False
6. Voice rights may be limited by: A. Use/territory/term B. File size only C. Sample rate only D. None
7. Synthetic speech should be: A. Always undisclosed B. Reviewed and disclosed where required C. Treated as authentic recording D. Used for impersonation
8. A fluent reviewer is important for: A. Translation quality B. Video frame rate C. Codec choice D. Disk speed
9. Delivery permission automatically grants portfolio use. A. True B. False
10. AI transcript is: A. A draft requiring review B. Guaranteed exact C. Legal proof D. A replacement for consent

## Part B — Short answer (5 × 3 = 15)
11. Distinguish transcription, translation and dubbing.
12. Name three items in a voice-rights record.
13. Why must names and numbers be checked against source audio?
14. What is the purpose of a pronunciation guide?
15. What should a professional speech handoff contain?

## Part C — Applied case (15)
16. A client supplies a recording of a well-known person and asks to clone the voice for a commercial endorsement. No permission is supplied. Explain the safe response and an acceptable alternative. (8)
17. An AI transcript changes a product dosage/quantity from the recording. Explain correction and review steps. (7)

## Answer key
1 B; 2 B; 3 B; 4 A; 5 B; 6 A; 7 B; 8 A; 9 B; 10 A.
11. Transcription records speech as text; translation transfers meaning to another language; dubbing replaces/overlays speech with a target performance. (3)
12. Speaker/owner, permitted uses, territory, term, channels, synthetic rights, disclosure and consent date. (3)
13. ASR errors can change meaning and material claims. (3)
14. It standardizes names, technical terms and pronunciation across takes. (3)
15. Approved masters, stems, scripts, transcripts, rights/consent, provenance, QC and manifest. (3)
16. Do not clone or publish; request explicit authorization and qualified rights review. Offer an original consenting actor or licensed synthetic voice that does not imitate the person. (8)
17. Replay source, correct transcript, verify context, log correction, recheck all translations/dubs and obtain human approval before delivery. (7)

## Performance bands
45–50 exemplary; 40–44 competent; 35–39 developing; below 35 remediation. Unauthorized impersonation, deceptive endorsement or privacy breach requires remediation regardless of score.
