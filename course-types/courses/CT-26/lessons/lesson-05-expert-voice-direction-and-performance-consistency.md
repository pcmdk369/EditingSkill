# CT-26 Lesson — Expert Voice Direction and Performance Consistency

- Course: CT-26 — AI Voice, Dubbing & Speech Workflows
- Level: Expert
- Estimated time: 90–150 minutes
- Prerequisites: Audio file handling and basic media literacy; see course prerequisite notes
- Tools/environment: Tool-neutral; record the actual audio editor, transcription/translation provider, version, OS and privacy terms used
- Version/review: Verify current provider terms, voice rights and destination requirements before each project

## Learning outcomes
By the end, learners can explain the core concepts, perform a reproducible workflow, identify failure modes and submit evidence suitable for review.

## Definition and terminology
Voice direction defines delivery, tone, pacing and emotional intention. Consistency means maintaining coherent character and recording quality across scenes and sessions.

## Purpose and professional relevance
Use approved direction notes and reference takes; preserve actor autonomy and avoid directing toward unsupported identity imitation.

## Tools and alternatives
Use a local audio editor/DAW and secure storage where possible. External transcription, translation or synthesis services must be reviewed for retention, model-training use, commercial rights, cost and permitted content. A spreadsheet and human review can replace a specialized platform for small projects. Never assume one provider's feature or license applies to another.

## Step-by-step procedure
Create a voice style sheet; record a reference passage; document mic distance, room, gain and delivery; compare takes; manage pickups; maintain pronunciation and terminology; obtain performer approval.

## Worked example
A pickup sounds louder and closer than the original. Match recording conditions or correct acoustically while preserving natural timbre.

## Guided practice
Build a voice continuity pack with reference take, pronunciation and recording notes.

## Independent assignment
Extend the practice to a new authorized recording or script. Record assumptions, tool versions, input identifiers, corrections, reviewer decisions and final output properties.

## Common errors and recovery
Using another person's voice as an imitation target without permission; inconsistent recording environment. Stop and obtain authorization or re-record.

## Assessment evidence
Submit direction sheet, reference recording, pickup log and continuity report.

## Knowledge check
1. Define the key concept in this lesson.
2. Identify one risk that requires human review.
3. Explain how you would verify the output against its source.
4. What evidence must be included in the handoff?

## Answer guidance
1. Use the definition above and distinguish it from adjacent speech workflows.
2. Consent, accuracy, privacy, language quality, identity or disclosure depending on the task.
3. Compare the complete output with the source recording/script and log corrections.
4. Source identity, tool/version, approvals, QC findings and final file properties.

## Quality-control checklist
- [ ] Source media and intended use are authorized.
- [ ] Provider terms/privacy reviewed before upload.
- [ ] Transcript/script and names checked by a human.
- [ ] Voice performance and sync reviewed.
- [ ] Synthetic content and required disclosure documented.
- [ ] Export format and audio properties match the brief.
- [ ] Version, approvals and archive details are traceable.

## Further study
Consult current official provider documentation, applicable voice/personality rights guidance and professional language-review standards. This lesson is educational and not legal advice.
