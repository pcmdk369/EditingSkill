# CT-16 Lesson 08 — Professional Music Delivery, Stems and Archive

- Course: CT-16 — Music Production & Audio Mixing
- Level: Professional Practice
- Estimated time: 120–180 minutes
- Prerequisites: Previous CT-16 lessons and authorized audio/MIDI assets
- Tools/environment: DAW, audio interface, monitoring and optional MIDI controller; record OS/app/version
- Version/review date: Tool-neutral; delivery and licensing requirements depend on project

## Learning outcomes
Deliver masters, instrumental/alternate mixes, stems and session files with rights, metadata, loudness and version documentation.

## Key definitions
- **DAW:** Digital audio workstation for recording, editing and mixing.
- **MIDI:** Performance/control data, not recorded audio itself.
- **Quantization:** Adjustment of note timing to a rhythmic grid.
- **Comping:** Selecting sections from multiple takes to form a composite performance.
- **Gain staging:** Managing signal levels through a signal path.
- **Bus:** Routing path combining multiple tracks.
- **Stem:** Aligned submix of related tracks.
- **True peak:** Estimate of reconstructed peak level.

## Purpose and theory
Professional delivery may include stereo master, instrumental, acapella, clean, stems, MIDI and project session. Each deliverable needs a clear naming convention, format and start/end alignment. Music rights and collaborator credits must be documented.

## Tools, requirements and alternatives
Use a current DAW and suitable audio interface/monitoring. Record sample rate, bit depth, plugin dependencies and session tempo/key. Use original or licensed audio, samples and instruments. Preserve raw takes and session backups.

## Procedure
1. Confirm deliverable list and format. 2. Reconcile composition, recording, sample and performer rights. 3. Check mix approval and version. 4. Export master and requested alternates. 5. Export stems with common start/end and naming. 6. Recombine stems to validate. 7. Measure loudness/true peak. 8. Package session/plugins/assets. 9. Create rights/credit manifest. 10. Archive and transfer securely.

## Worked example
A client receives WAV master, instrumental, acapella and aligned stems, plus session file and sample/rights register.

## Dry run
A stem is missing a reverb return. Inspect routing and re-export the affected stem, then recombine to confirm.

## Guided practice
Prepare a delivery package for a 60-second music cue.

## Independent assignment
Apply the workflow to a second arrangement or mix. Submit editable session, source assets, export, rights log and QC report.

## Common errors and recovery
| Symptom | Likely cause | Diagnosis | Recovery |
|---|---|---|---|
| Submit masters, alternates, stems, session, rights log, QC and manifest. | Production issue | Inspect source, routing and specification | Correct and retest |

## Knowledge check
1. How do tempo and meter differ?
2. What is MIDI data compared with audio?
3. Why should static balance precede heavy processing?
4. What should a mastering target be based on?
5. Why must stems be recombined for validation?

## Answer guidance
1. Tempo is pulse rate; meter groups beats into bars.
2. MIDI describes performance/control; audio is recorded/rendered sound.
3. It establishes balance before corrective processing.
4. The actual client/platform delivery specification.
5. It confirms routing, alignment and completeness.

## Quality-control checklist
- [ ] Session and source media preserved.
- [ ] Track names, buses and versions clear.
- [ ] Arrangement and edits retain intended musical expression.
- [ ] Mix checked for clipping, masking and mono issues.
- [ ] Final export measured and played.
- [ ] Samples, collaborators and AI use documented.
- [ ] Stems and archive validated.
