# CT-02 Lesson 04 — Continuity Editing and Match on Action

- Course: CT-02 — Film & Cinematic Editing
- Level: Beginner → Intermediate
- Estimated time: 3 hours
- Prerequisites: CT-02 Lessons 01–03 recommended; ability to navigate a timeline frame by frame
- Tools/environment: NLE with frame stepping and source viewer; original or licensed footage
- Version/review date: 1.0 — 2026-11-16

## Learning outcomes
Learners can:
- explain continuity editing and its relationship to audience comprehension;
- compare action, prop, body, eyeline and sound continuity across adjacent shots;
- construct a match-on-action cut using overlapping source coverage;
- diagnose jump cuts and mismatched action states;
- distinguish intentional discontinuity from an accidental continuity error.

## Key definitions and terminology
| Term | Definition | Context/example |
|---|---|---|
| Continuity editing | Editing that maintains coherent time, space and action across cuts | Door opening continues across angles |
| Match on action | Cut between views during one continuous movement | Hand reaches in medium, grips handle in close-up |
| Action state | Exact stage of an action at a moment | Door closed, partly open, fully open |
| Prop continuity | Consistency in object position/state | Cup remains in same hand |
| Screen direction | Apparent movement direction across frame | Person continues left-to-right |
| Jump cut | Noticeable discontinuity within a continuous view/action | Hand jumps position between adjacent frames |
| Overlap | Shared portion of action covered by two shots | Both angles include hand reaching |
| Cut point | Exact frame/time at which one shot changes to another | Cut during the reach |

## Purpose and professional relevance
Continuity helps viewers understand action without mentally reconstructing missing spatial or temporal information. Match on action can make a cut feel natural because the viewer follows the movement rather than noticing the change in camera angle. The technique is used in dialogue, action, product demonstrations, documentary sequences and instructional media.

## Theory and principles
A successful match-on-action cut coordinates:
- movement direction and speed;
- body and hand position;
- object state;
- timing and gesture phase;
- camera angle and screen geography;
- sound continuity where relevant.

The editor usually cuts during the action, not before it begins or after it has completely stopped. The first shot establishes the movement; the second completes or continues it. A small overlap may hide the transition, but excessive overlap can create repeated movement. Insufficient overlap can create a visible jump.

Continuity is not a demand that every cut be invisible. A jump cut, temporal ellipsis or fragmented action may be intentional. The distinction is whether the discontinuity supports the scene's meaning and is legible as a deliberate choice.

## Tools, requirements and alternatives
- **Required:** two or more angles covering the same action; NLE with frame stepping.
- **Optional:** waveform display, clip markers, comparison viewer, proxy media.
- **Alternatives:** frame contact sheets or a written frame-by-frame action log.
- **Safety:** preserve camera originals and work on copies; do not overwrite source media.

## Procedure
1. Identify one continuous physical action, such as reaching for and opening a door.
2. Locate all angles that cover the action and verify their chronological relationship.
3. Mark the start, contact, peak and completion of the movement in each angle.
4. Compare hand/body position and object state at candidate cut points.
5. Confirm both shots preserve the same screen direction and scene geography.
6. Place the first shot on the timeline and trim near the start of the action.
7. Place the second shot so it continues the action from a compatible phase.
8. Adjust the cut frame by frame. Avoid repeating a visible movement or skipping a critical contact.
9. Listen for sound discontinuity. Use production sound or a properly licensed effect only when needed and accurately represented.
10. Watch at normal speed, then inspect frame by frame.
11. Test on a second display or export a review file if the cut is difficult to evaluate in the NLE viewer.
12. Record the selected source ranges and reason for the cut.

## Worked example
**Brief/input:** Shot A shows a person reaching for a door handle in a medium shot. Shot B shows a close-up of the hand turning the handle.

**Decision process:** Find the frame in Shot A where the hand begins the reach and the frame in Shot B where the hand is at a compatible position. Cut while the hand is moving. Ensure the handle is in the same state and that the hand does not teleport or reverse direction. Use the sound of contact consistently; do not duplicate the handle click.

**Expected output:** A cut in which the viewer perceives one continuous reach-and-turn action across two angles.

## Dry run / annotated walkthrough
| Action phase | Shot A | Shot B | Editorial check |
|---|---|---|---|
| Start | Arm begins moving | Arm/hand already moving | Do not cut to a later phase too early |
| Approach | Hand nears handle | Hand approaches handle | Match direction and speed |
| Contact | Fingers touch handle | Fingers touch handle | Align contact moment |
| Turn | Wrist rotates | Handle rotates | Avoid duplicated or skipped rotation |
| Completion | Door begins opening | Door continues opening | Confirm door state and movement |

If Shot B begins after the door is already open, the cut may skip the handle turn. If it begins too early, the viewer may see the reach repeated. Choose the best available overlap and document any unavoidable mismatch.

## Guided practice
Record or use a permitted sequence of a person picking up a cup. Obtain a wide/medium shot and a close detail. Edit the reach, grip and lift as one continuous action. Ask a viewer to identify the cut. If they notice it, ask whether the issue is timing, position, direction, sound or geography.

**Success criteria:** the cup does not jump, the hand movement is coherent, and the cut does not repeat or skip the essential action.

## Independent assignment
Create a 30–60 second continuity sequence containing three actions: pick up an object, move it to another location, and place it down. Use at least two angles for one action. Submit:
- source shot/timecode log;
- action-state table;
- final edit;
- three annotated frames around the match-on-action cut;
- short note explaining one continuity problem and its correction.

## Common errors and recovery
| Symptom | Likely cause | Diagnosis | Recovery |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hand jumps forward/backward | Incompatible action phase | Compare contact and movement frames | Shift cut point or choose another take |
| Movement reverses | Screen direction mismatch | Trace movement through both shots | Use compatible angle or show turn |
| Object changes state | Prop continuity mismatch | Inspect object at cut | Select matching coverage or omit detail |
| Action repeats | Too much overlap | Watch at normal speed | Remove duplicated movement |
| Sound clicks twice or drops | Production sound overlap/gap | Inspect waveform and listen | Use one clean event and smooth ambience |
| Cut is visible for the wrong reason | Angle/scale mismatch or poor timing | Watch at normal speed first | Reposition cut or choose a better angle |

## Knowledge check
1. Why is a match-on-action cut usually placed during movement?
2. Name four conditions that should match across the cut.
3. What is the risk of excessive overlap?
4. How can an intentional jump cut differ from an accidental continuity error?

## Answer key / instructor guidance
1. Movement directs attention and helps mask the change in camera angle.
2. Direction, movement phase, body/hand position, object state, geography and sound.
3. The movement may appear to repeat.
4. Intentional discontinuity has a clear expressive purpose; accidental error undermines comprehension without serving the story.

## Assessment rubric
| Criterion | Points | Evidence required |
|---|---:|---|
| Action analysis | 20 | Start/contact/peak/completion identified |
| Cut timing | 25 | Movement continues naturally |
| Spatial and prop continuity | 20 | Position, direction and object state match |
| Sound continuity | 15 | No distracting gap or duplicated event |
| Documentation and reflection | 20 | Source ranges, annotated frames and diagnosis |
**Pass threshold:** 70/100; no critical continuity error that makes the action impossible to understand.

## Quality-control checklist
- [ ] Source angles cover the same real action.
- [ ] Cut occurs at a compatible movement phase.
- [ ] Hand/body position and object state are consistent.
- [ ] Screen direction and geography remain legible.
- [ ] Sound does not duplicate or interrupt the action.
- [ ] Project and source media remain intact.
