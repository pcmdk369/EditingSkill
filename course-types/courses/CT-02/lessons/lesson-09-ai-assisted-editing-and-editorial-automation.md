# CT-02 Lesson 09 — AI-Assisted Editing and Editorial Automation

- Course: CT-02 — Film & Cinematic Editing
- Level: AI & Automation
- Estimated time: 3–4 hours
- Prerequisites: Lessons 01–08; basic understanding of source media and editorial intent
- Environment: Optional transcription, scene-detection, search or NLE automation tools; verify availability and terms
- Version/review date: 1.0 — 2026-11-16

## Learning outcomes
Learners can select an appropriate AI-assisted task, protect source media and private information, evaluate model output, maintain human editorial control, and document automation decisions.

## Terminology
- **Speech-to-text (STT):** automatic conversion of speech audio into text.
- **Scene detection:** automated identification of possible shot/scene boundaries.
- **Semantic search:** retrieval of media based on descriptive meaning rather than exact filename.
- **Proxy:** lower-resource copy used for editing; it is not the camera original.
- **Human-in-the-loop:** a workflow in which a person reviews and approves consequential model output.
- **Provenance:** record of source, transformations, tools and approvals.

## Theory and risk
AI can accelerate transcription, logging, rough selects, translation drafts, shot search and repetitive operations. It can also hallucinate words, misidentify speakers, miss context, alter meaning, introduce temporal inconsistencies or upload sensitive media to third parties. Automation is a candidate generator, not editorial authority. Preserve the original and retain a traceable link between suggested edits and source timecodes.

## Procedure
1. Define one narrow task and a measurable success criterion.
2. Check asset ownership, participant consent, confidentiality and tool data-handling terms.
3. Prefer local processing for sensitive material where practical and authorized.
4. Test a short, non-sensitive sample before processing a full project.
5. Generate draft transcript, markers, selects or metadata; preserve confidence/uncertainty indicators if available.
6. Compare every important transcript segment against source audio; correct names, numbers, negation, technical terms and speaker attribution.
7. Verify scene/shot boundaries by viewing source frames; automated cuts may be false positives.
8. Keep original timecodes and source IDs attached to every suggestion.
9. Have the editor make final performance, story, pacing and representation decisions.
10. Record tool/model/version, date, input scope, settings, human corrections and unresolved uncertainty.
11. Re-check the final timeline for sync, continuity, rights and unintended synthetic alterations.

## Worked example
A 12-minute interview is transcribed to identify a 20-second answer. Search the draft transcript for relevant terms, then listen to the source around the suggested timecode. If the transcript omitted “not,” the meaning reverses; correct the transcript before selecting the clip. Confirm the edit against the full question and response.

## Dry run
Transcribe a 60-second self-recorded clip. Mark each error category: omission, substitution, punctuation, speaker, timestamp or translation. Calculate word accuracy only if a verified reference transcript exists; otherwise report qualitative error counts.

## Guided practice
Use an authorized interview clip. Produce a transcript with source timecodes, correct it manually, and select a short answer while preserving context.

## Independent assignment
Design an AI-assisted logging workflow with a privacy note, test plan, error log, human review checklist and fallback manual process.

## Common errors
| Error | Why it matters | Recovery |
|---|---|---|
| Trusting transcript blindly | Meaning can be reversed | Listen against source |
| Uploading private footage without approval | Privacy/contract breach | Stop; use approved local workflow |
| Using AI selects as final edit | Context/performance may be lost | Human editorial review |
| Losing source timecodes | Suggestions become untraceable | Retain source IDs and offsets |

## Knowledge check
1. Is AI transcript a verified record? — No; it requires source review.
2. Why preserve timecodes? — Traceability and accurate retrieval.
3. Who approves final creative decisions? — The authorized human editor/director.

## Assessment (100)
Task selection 15; privacy/rights assessment 20; reproducible test 15; accuracy review 25; provenance and fallback 15; final human decision record 10. Pass 80; any unauthorized sensitive upload is an automatic fail.

## QC checklist
- [ ] Task and success criteria defined.
- [ ] Data handling and rights reviewed.
- [ ] Sample test completed.
- [ ] Output checked against source.
- [ ] Human decisions and corrections documented.

## Version notes
Tool capabilities and data policies change. Verify official terms and model/version at time of use; no particular service is endorsed or assumed available.
