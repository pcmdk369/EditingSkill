# CT-02 Capstone — Three-to-Five-Minute Narrative Scene

- Level: Master / Professional Practice
- Estimated time: 12–18 hours
- Status: Capstone brief authored; learner execution and independent review not evidenced
- Textbook: [Chapter 12 — Narrative Scene Capstone](../CT-02-FILM-CINEMATIC-EDITING-TEXTBOOK.md)
- Evidence workbook: [Section 22 — Narrative Scene Capstone Evidence Pack](../CT-02-STUDENT-NOTES-WORKBOOK.md#22-narrative-scene-capstone-evidence-pack)
- Assessment record: [Section 26 — Knowledge Assessment Blueprint, Scoring and Remediation](../CT-02-STUDENT-NOTES-WORKBOOK.md#26-knowledge-assessment-blueprint-scoring-and-remediation)

## Brief
Edit an original or authorized 3–5 minute narrative scene from supplied coverage. Establish clear geography, preserve performance, maintain screen direction, shape dramatic pacing and deliver an approved picture-locked cut with documented finishing handoff.

## Learning outcomes

On successful completion, the learner can:
- map a scene objective, conflict, dramatic beats and ending change to authorized source evidence;
- select coverage and performances while documenting source ranges and editorial alternatives;
- maintain or intentionally motivate geography, eyelines, screen direction and continuity;
- shape pacing and sound transitions to support comprehension and dramatic intent;
- progress through assembly, rough cut, fine cut and an approved, version-specific picture lock;
- deliver an inspected master with a written specification, QC record and traceable handoff.

## Prerequisites and setup

- Study CT-02 Lessons 01–10 and review the four practical labs. The capstone integrates those competencies; it is not a replacement for their instruction.
- Have an approved brief and original or authorized source media, or clearly label a paper-only planning submission.
- Record the selected application, version, edition and relevant project settings if software is used. No particular NLE is mandated.
- Identify the person authorized to approve creative, factual, rights and final-delivery decisions.
- If authorized footage or a suitable device is unavailable, complete the planning and paper-edit components only. Do not claim a timeline, export, playback, portability or restore test was executed.


## Required source material
- scene brief and script;
- authorized dialogue and camera coverage;
- slate/timecode or sync reference;
- B-roll/insert material where appropriate;
- music/sound assets with documented rights;
- production notes and any continuity restrictions.

## Required deliverables
1. Scene breakdown and dramatic beat map.
2. Coverage/shot log with source IDs and timecodes.
3. Screen-direction and geography diagram.
4. Paper edit or assembly map.
5. Rough cut and fine cut with version notes.
6. Continuity report (eyelines, props, action, wardrobe and screen direction).
7. Multicamera sync report if applicable.
8. Picture-lock approval and change log.
9. Final export, captions if required and technical QC report.
10. Editable project with media dependencies and handoff manifest.
11. 500–800 word editorial reflection.

## Mandatory gates
No unauthorized source use, fabricated dialogue, misleading performance construction or unapproved music. Preserve originals. A picture lock must have a traceable approval and controlled change process.

## Rubric — 100 points

Use the performance descriptors to select an evidence-based score within each criterion's maximum. The descriptors are not a substitute for the mandatory gates below.

| Criterion | Max | Strong (90–100% of criterion) | Competent (75–89%) | Developing (60–74%) | Not demonstrated (0–59%) |
|---|---:|---|---|---|---|
| Dramatic structure and clarity | 15 | Objective, conflict, turning point and ending change are clear; beats trace to source; no unsupported causal implication. | Story is understandable; one beat or transition is weaker but does not obscure the arc. | Objective or turning point is partly unclear; a causal or informational gap remains. | Story cannot be followed or beat claims lack source evidence. |
| Screen direction and scene geography | 15 | Axis, positions, eyelines and movement are consistently clear or intentionally motivated; map matches the edit. | Geography is mostly clear with one minor, non-disruptive mismatch. | Repeated spatial uncertainty or incomplete mapping distracts. | No coherent spatial relationship or the map materially contradicts the edit. |
| Coverage and performance selection | 15 | Shot function and performance choices are purposeful, source-traceable and preserve recorded meaning; alternatives are explained. | Suitable coverage and performances are used; some selection rationale is incomplete. | Selection relies mainly on appearance or leaves important performance/coverage gaps. | Selects are untraceable or the edit materially misrepresents recorded performance. |
| Continuity and match on action | 15 | Action phases, eyelines, props, wardrobe, performance and sound are checked; match-on-action boundaries are readable or intentionally motivated. | Continuity is generally sound with a minor issue that does not alter meaning. | Repeated mismatches distract or an important action boundary is weak. | Action, chronology or continuity is incoherent or misleading. |
| Pacing and emotional rhythm | 10 | Duration and internal/external rhythm serve beats and comprehension; choices are explained with evidence. | Rhythm generally supports the scene with minor unevenness. | Timing is mechanical or inconsistent; rationale is weak. | Pacing materially obstructs comprehension or dramatic intent. |
| Audio/dialogue and synchronization | 10 | Dialogue is intelligible; sync, channel routing and transitions are checked and documented. | Audio is usable with a minor, non-critical issue documented. | An unresolved level, sync or continuity issue remains. | Dialogue is unusable or a material sync failure is unaddressed. |
| Picture lock and version governance | 5 | Exact lock ID, authorized approval and post-lock change record are complete. | Approval and version are identifiable with a minor record gap. | Version or approval authority is ambiguous. | No traceable approval or an unapproved post-lock change is presented as final. |
| Technical delivery and QC | 10 | Written specification matches actual file properties; complete playback, defect correction and retest are evidenced. | Required file checks are evidenced with a minor documentation gap. | Inspection is partial or a significant defect remains unresolved. | No actual file inspection is evidenced or a false QC claim is made. |
| Project organization and reflection | 5 | Manifest, dependencies, handoff restrictions and evidence-based reflection are complete. | Package is usable with a minor missing record. | Dependencies or reflection are incomplete. | Project cannot be understood/handed off or reflection is absent. |
| **Total** | **100** | | | | |

**Pass:** 80/100 or higher, with every mandatory gate passed. Rights, consent, source preservation, truthful evidence, authorized approval and actual-file QC are non-compensable gates; they are not offset by creative points.

**Scoring rules:** Assessors may award whole-number points within each criterion maximum based on the evidence and descriptor. Missing evidence is Not Demonstrated. A paper-only submission may receive credit for planning and analysis but receives no execution, export, full-playback, portability or restore credit. The capstone cannot pass until required execution evidence is demonstrated.


## Review protocol
Reviewer watches the full scene, traces a sample of edits to source timecodes, checks continuity notes, verifies rights and sync, inspects export metadata and confirms project portability. This brief alone does not validate execution.


## 11. Seven stage gates and exit evidence

Use Pass, Conditional or Fail. Conditional decisions name the missing evidence, owner and due condition.

| Gate | Work | Required evidence | Pass condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| G0 — Authorization and brief | Scope, creative intent, rights, consent and delivery | Approved brief, asset inventory, rights register, acceptance matrix | Source use is authorized and requirements are testable |
| G1 — Editorial plan | Beats, POV, coverage, geography and continuity risks | Beat sheet, selects log, axis diagram, source references | Major beats have source support or a disclosed gap |
| G2 — Assembly | Build complete story order | Assembly/paper edit, issue log, version ID | Story is understandable and gaps are visible |
| G3 — Rough cut | Test structure, performance, rhythm and continuity | Review copy, notes/disposition log, change rationale | Review decisions are assigned to authorized owner |
| G4 — Fine cut and picture lock | Refine, resolve issues and control changes | Fine-cut ID, approval/sign-off, lock record | Exact approved picture version is identifiable |
| G5 — Finishing and delivery | Conform, sound, titles/captions, export and inspect | Delivery matrix, export record, actual master, full-playback QC | Actual file passes required specification |
| G6 — Handoff and reflection | Package project, dependencies, rights and lessons | Manifest, relink notes, portability/restore evidence if executed, reflection | Contents, restrictions, version and dependencies are clear |

## 12. Acceptance tests and mandatory remediation

**Creative:** objective, conflict and ending change are understandable; dialogue meaning and performance are preserved; geography and continuity are clear or intentionally motivated; pacing supports comprehension; sound and dialogue are intelligible and synchronized.

**Technical:** actual export properties match the written specification; the actual file is played from beginning to end; defects are corrected in a new version and rechecked; dependencies are documented; portability is claimed only after reopening a separate copy.

**Professional:** rights, consent, credits, privacy and permitted use are documented; approval is attributed to the correct authority and exact version; originals and prior approved versions are preserved; simulated or unexecuted evidence is labeled honestly.

**Mandatory remediation:** unauthorized source use, destructive overwrite, fabricated dialogue/evidence, unsupported factual implication, unapproved post-lock change, untraceable source identity, knowingly false technical claim or QC pass reported without full-file inspection. These cannot be offset by rubric points.

## 13. Reviewer protocol and resubmission

1. Verify brief, rights and source inventory before viewing.
2. Watch the full scene once without interruption.
3. Trace sample edits to source ranges, including dialogue, continuity-sensitive action and the turning point.
4. Match lock ID to submitted export and approval record.
5. Inspect actual export properties and complete playback.
6. Reopen a separate project copy if portability is claimed.
7. Score only observable evidence; mark missing evidence as **not demonstrated**.
8. Record critical-gate results and criterion-specific remediation.

For each failed criterion, record defect, severity, evidence, required action, owner and resubmission evidence. Use a new version ID and repeat affected checks, including regression checks for sound, captions, titles, credits, runtime, derivatives and metadata. Preserve the prior submission and review record.

## 14. Evidence integrity and paper-only alternative

A paper-only submission may demonstrate analysis, beat mapping, source planning and editorial reasoning. It must be labeled **paper-only / not an executed edit**. It cannot receive execution, export, portability or playback-QC credit. Do not fabricate screenshots, approvals, timecodes, file metadata, learner submissions or restore evidence. A plan to perform a restore is not a restore test.

## 15. Rubric moderation and score record

1. Confirm the brief, authorized source inventory, submitted version IDs and any declared paper-only or simulated evidence before scoring.
2. Watch the complete scene before sampling individual edits. Trace representative decisions to source ranges and the learner's records.
3. Score each criterion independently against the descriptors. Cite an artifact, version, source range or time reference for any material deduction or mandatory hold.
4. Distinguish a creative weakness from missing evidence, a technical defect and a rights/approval failure. Mark absent execution evidence Not Demonstrated.
5. Where reviewers disagree, compare the evidence and document the final rationale. Do not silently average scores or treat an unverified claim as proof.
6. Record total score, mandatory gate results, reviewer, date, feedback and remediation status in Workbook Section 22.13 and the assessment record in Section 26.

**Reviewer score record:** criterion scores /100; mandatory gates (Pass / Hold); version IDs reviewed; evidence references; remediation required; final decision (Pass / Remediation Required / Not Demonstrated).
