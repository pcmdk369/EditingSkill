# CT-02 — Knowledge Assessment and Answer Guidance

**Course:** Film & Cinematic Editing  
**Assessment type:** Closed-notes knowledge check followed by practical evidence review  
**Suggested time:** 45–60 minutes  
**Pass threshold:** 70% overall; critical rights/safety and source-preservation requirements must also be met.

## Part A — Multiple choice (10 × 2 = 20 points)

Choose one answer.

1. What is the most useful definition of a dramatic beat?
   - A. Any cut between two clips
   - B. A meaningful change in information, intention or emotion
   - C. A fixed five-second shot
   - D. A camera movement

2. The 180-degree guideline primarily helps preserve:
   - A. Audio loudness
   - B. Color consistency
   - C. Screen geography and left-right relationships
   - D. Export file size

3. A match on action is most effective when:
   - A. The movement continues plausibly across the cut
   - B. The subject changes direction without explanation
   - C. The second shot begins after the action is complete
   - D. The editor adds a dissolve to every cut

4. Which shot commonly establishes broad scene geography?
   - A. Insert
   - B. Master shot
   - C. Extreme close-up
   - D. Reaction detail

5. A J-cut occurs when:
   - A. Current audio continues over the next picture
   - B. Next-scene audio begins before its picture
   - C. Picture begins before all audio
   - D. A clip is reversed

6. What does sync drift mean?
   - A. A fixed offset at the start
   - B. A changing timing difference over the recording
   - C. A color shift between cameras
   - D. A change in shot size

7. Which statement about pacing is correct?
   - A. Faster cuts always mean faster pacing
   - B. Every dialogue line should be shortened
   - C. Pacing includes meaningful changes within shots as well as cut frequency
   - D. Long shots cannot create tension

8. What is the main purpose of picture lock?
   - A. To permanently prevent all future changes
   - B. To establish an approved picture timing for downstream work
   - C. To finalize every sound and color decision automatically
   - D. To delete unused media

9. Which is an actionable editorial note?
   - A. “Make it better.”
   - B. “More cinematic.”
   - C. “At v004, timeline 00:00:42:10, hold the listener's reaction longer after the reveal; compare take 2.”
   - D. “Fix everything.”

10. What is the strongest proof that an export meets a delivery brief?
    - A. The preset name contains “Master”
    - B. The timeline looks correct
    - C. The rendered file is inspected and its properties verified against the brief
    - D. The export dialog closed without an error

## Part B — Short answer (5 × 4 = 20 points)

11. Explain the difference between story and plot.
12. Give two ways to make an intentional axis crossing understandable.
13. Name four factors to inspect when selecting between alternate performance takes.
14. Explain why synchronization should be checked near the start, middle and end of a long multicamera recording.
15. Name four items that belong in a professional finishing handoff.

## Part C — Scenario analysis (3 × 10 = 30 points)

16. A dialogue scene has Character A looking screen-right and Character B looking screen-left. One close-up of Character B looks screen-right. Explain the checks you would perform before deciding whether to use or reject the shot.

17. Two camera angles align at the start of a 20-minute interview but are visibly out of sync near the end. Describe a diagnostic sequence and list possible causes. Distinguish a fixed offset from drift.

18. A director requests a picture change after picture lock. Describe the change-control record you would create and identify at least four downstream departments or deliverables that may be affected.

## Part D — Practical evidence review (30 points)

Submit evidence from the four CT-02 labs:
- scene beat sheet and coverage/geography map (8);
- continuity edit and report (7);
- synchronized multicamera sequence and three-point sync check (7);
- picture-lock/handoff package and completed full-file QC report (8).

## Answer guidance

### Part A
1. B — A beat is a meaningful change.
2. C — The guideline maintains screen geography.
3. A — Movement must continue plausibly.
4. B — A master commonly establishes broad geography and action.
5. B — A J-cut introduces the next audio before its picture.
6. B — Drift changes over time.
7. C — Pacing includes internal and external rhythm.
8. B — Picture lock provides an approved timing basis for finishing.
9. C — It identifies version, timecode, desired change and a comparison.
10. C — Verify the actual rendered file, not only a preset or timeline.

### Part B — Expected points
11. Story is the events understood; plot is the order and manner in which the film presents selected events.
12. Examples: neutral shot, visible camera move across the axis, motivated character movement, clear re-establishing wide shot.
13. Performance truth, continuity, focus/exposure, timing/handles, dialogue/sound quality, consistency with scene tone.
14. Multiple checks reveal whether the timing relationship changes; start-only alignment can hide clock/timebase mismatch or variable-frame-rate issues.
15. Examples: approved master, project/interchange, media inventory/relink instructions, graphics, rights records, change log, delivery specification, QC report.

### Part C — Marking guidance
16. Full credit requires checking camera placement relative to axis, character positions, actual gaze direction, scene diagram, neighboring shots and whether the apparent reversal is motivated.
17. Full credit distinguishes constant offset from increasing/decreasing offset and checks frame/timebase, variable frame rate, sample rate, recording interruptions and clock accuracy; recommends documenting measurements and using an appropriate correction.
18. Full credit includes sequence/version, timecode, reason, requester, approval, change description, affected assets and downstream notification. Potential impacts include sound mix, music, VFX, color, subtitles/captions, credits and platform versions.

## Critical failure conditions
- Unlicensed or unauthorized source material is knowingly used.
- Original source media is destructively overwritten or discarded.
- A technical compliance claim is knowingly false.
- A delivered file is not inspected but is reported as fully QC-passed.
