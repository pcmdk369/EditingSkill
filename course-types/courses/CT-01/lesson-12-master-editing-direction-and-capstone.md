# CT-01 — Master: Editorial Direction, Pipeline Design and Master Capstone

**Course:** Video Editing  
**Pathway stage:** Master

## Learning outcomes
Develop an editorial treatment; define a consistent visual and sound language; design a scalable workflow; direct revisions; defend creative decisions; deliver a polished multi-format master package.

## Prerequisites
Lessons 01–11 and successful intermediate/advanced exercises.

## Key definitions
- Editorial treatment: concise description of story approach, structure, tone, pacing, visual language and sound.
- Visual language: consistent use of framing, movement, color, composition and transitions.
- Motif: recurring image, sound or idea that builds meaning.
- Pipeline: ordered workflow connecting people, assets, tools, approvals and deliverables.
- Master: approved high-quality deliverable used as the source for distribution versions, subject to the agreed specification.
- Derivative: version created from a master for a different platform, aspect ratio, language or use.
- QC gate: required inspection point before work proceeds to the next stage.
- Risk register: list of project risks, likelihood, impact and mitigation.
- Handoff manifest: inventory of delivered files, versions, specifications and checksums where used.

## Why it matters
Mastery is not the use of the largest number of effects. It is the ability to make intentional editorial decisions, design a reliable workflow and deliver work that meets a brief under real constraints.

## Theory
An editorial treatment aligns the team before detailed editing. It should describe audience, central idea, narrative structure, pacing, shot language, sound approach, color direction, accessibility and deliverables.

A production pipeline should include review gates: ingest verification, story approval, rough-cut approval, picture lock, finishing review, master QC and archive verification. A failure at a gate should return the project to the appropriate earlier stage rather than being hidden downstream.

Derivatives should be created from the approved master or a controlled project version, not by repeatedly compressing an already compressed social export. Keep creative intent while adapting safe areas, captions, text size and pacing to each format.

## Tools and alternatives
Any NLE, audio/color tools, project documentation, asset storage and checksum utility. A small project can use a clear folder structure and spreadsheet; a larger project may need shared storage, review systems and formal asset management.

## Procedure
1. Analyze the brief and identify audience, message, constraints and acceptance criteria.
2. Write an editorial treatment and reference board using owned or licensed material.
3. Create a story outline, selects plan and sequence strategy.
4. Design folder structure, naming convention, backups, versioning and review gates.
5. Assign roles and approval ownership if working with a team.
6. Build a rough cut that tests story before detailed finishing.
7. Run structured reviews and record decisions.
8. Lock picture, then complete sound, color, titles, captions and accessibility.
9. Create the approved master and inspect it against technical specifications.
10. Derive landscape and vertical versions from the controlled master/project.
11. Check each derivative for reframing, captions, graphics, pacing and platform overlays.
12. Package editable project, media manifest, exports, approvals, version log and archive.
13. Verify archive readability and restore a sample project copy.

## Worked example
A 75-second profile of a local craft maker is designed for landscape web viewing and vertical mobile viewing. The treatment uses observational close-ups, natural sound and a restrained title system. The vertical version is reframed shot by shot; titles are repositioned and captions enlarged. It is not created by blindly cropping the landscape export.

## Dry run
Create a one-page treatment and pipeline map. Mark each gate with: required input, reviewer, acceptance test, output and recovery path. Example: ingest gate requires copied media, inventory and successful playback; failure returns to ingest.

## Guided exercise
Build a 30-second proof-of-concept in two aspect ratios. Present the treatment, pipeline and QC gates before showing the outputs.

## Independent master capstone
Create a 60–90 second original story in landscape and vertical versions. Required deliverables:
- signed-off brief and editorial treatment;
- rights-cleared asset inventory;
- organized editable project and source media manifest;
- selects log and story outline;
- rough-cut and revision records;
- final picture-locked sequence;
- final sound and color notes;
- accessible captions and verified title spelling;
- landscape and vertical exports;
- technical QC report and viewing notes;
- version/change log and archive manifest.

### Capstone rubric — 100 points
| Criterion | Points | Master standard |
|---|---:|---|
| Story and editorial direction | 20 | Clear, engaging, coherent and purposeful |
| Editing craft and continuity | 15 | Precise pacing, transitions and spatial logic |
| Sound and finishing | 15 | Intelligible, balanced and consistent |
| Color and visual language | 10 | Controlled, coherent and intentional |
| Multi-format adaptation | 10 | Both formats feel deliberately composed |
| Rights, accessibility and ethics | 10 | Assets authorized, captions accurate, claims/context fair |
| Project organization and versioning | 10 | Editable, traceable and reproducible |
| QC, delivery and reflection | 10 | Meets brief, validates exports and explains decisions |

Pass: 85/100 with no critical rights, privacy, synchronization, corruption or delivery-spec failure. A critical failure requires correction regardless of total score.

## Knowledge check
1. What belongs in an editorial treatment?
2. Why use QC gates?
3. Why should vertical derivatives be reframed rather than simply cropped?
4. What is a handoff manifest?
5. What evidence demonstrates mastery beyond a polished export?

## Answer key
1. Audience, message, structure, tone, pacing, visual/sound approach, accessibility and deliverables.
2. They detect failures early and define recovery paths.
3. Composition, subject placement, titles and captions differ by format.
4. Inventory of delivered files, versions, specifications and verification information.
5. Editable project, documented decisions, rights records, version history, QC and reproducible delivery.

## Final quality-control checklist
- [ ] Brief and treatment approved.
- [ ] Rights and permissions documented.
- [ ] Source media backed up and inventoried.
- [ ] Project opens without missing media.
- [ ] Story and picture lock approved.
- [ ] Dialogue, music and effects reviewed.
- [ ] Color and titles checked across shots.
- [ ] Captions synchronized and proofread.
- [ ] Landscape and vertical versions reviewed independently.
- [ ] Exports match delivery specifications.
- [ ] Project, manifests and archive are complete.
- [ ] Restore test succeeds.

## Further study
Study editorial leadership, directing, advanced color science, sound post, delivery engineering, production management and portfolio presentation.
