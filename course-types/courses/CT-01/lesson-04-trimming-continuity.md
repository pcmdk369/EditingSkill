# CT-01 Lesson 04 — Trimming, Ripple Editing and Continuity

- Stage: Beginner to Intermediate
- Practice time: 90–120 minutes
- Prerequisites: Lesson 03
- Assets: Rough assembly containing at least five shots

## Learning outcomes
Shorten and extend edits; distinguish common trim operations; close unwanted gaps; preserve synchronization; improve visual continuity.

## Key definitions
- Trim: adjust a clip's start or end.
- Head/tail: beginning/end of a clip.
- Ripple delete: removes a clip or gap and shifts later material to close the space.
- Roll edit: moves the boundary between adjacent clips while preserving total sequence duration.
- Slip edit: changes source frames shown inside a clip without changing its timeline position or duration.
- Slide edit: moves a clip while adjusting neighboring clip edges to preserve surrounding continuity.
- Gap: empty timeline region.
- Continuity: consistency of action, screen direction, timing and spatial relationships.
- Cutaway: related shot that temporarily shows detail or context.
- Match on action: cut during a continuous movement so it appears seamless.

## Why this matters
A rough assembly often contains pauses, repeated actions and weak boundaries. Trimming improves rhythm; continuity helps viewers understand the action and space.

## Theory
An edit point determines when one shot changes to the next. A cut too early may remove needed information; a cut too late may leave dead time. Review context before and after the edit, not only the exact frame.

Ripple changes duration and moves downstream clips. Roll shifts a boundary between adjacent clips. Slip changes the source range inside a fixed timeline clip. Slide repositions a clip while adjusting its neighbors. Names and exact behavior can vary by NLE.

## Procedure
1. Duplicate or checkpoint the rough assembly.
2. Play at normal speed and mark sections that feel too long, short or confusing.
3. Trim unwanted material from a clip head or tail.
4. Use ripple delete only when downstream material should close the gap.
5. Use a roll edit when the boundary between two adjacent shots needs to move but total sequence duration should remain unchanged.
6. Use a slip edit when the clip's timeline position and duration are right but the visible source action is wrong.
7. Use a slide edit when a clip should move while neighboring edges compensate.
8. Check screen direction, action phase, object/hand position, eyelines, lighting and background.
9. Review with handles before and after each cut.
10. Play the entire sequence at normal speed.

## Worked example
A wide shot shows a person placing a cup on a table. The close-up begins before the cup reaches the table, making the action jump backward. Adjust the edit point so both shots show compatible phases of the action. Review at normal speed.

## Dry run
Two adjacent clips are 10 seconds and 6 seconds long. A roll edit transfers one second from the first to the second: durations become 9 and 7 seconds; combined duration remains 16 seconds. A ripple trim that removes one second shifts later content earlier and reduces sequence duration by one second unless another operation compensates.

## Guided practice
Find three weak edit points. Improve each with the least disruptive trim operation. Record original/revised timecodes and explain why the cut improved.

## Independent assignment
Deliver a refined 30–45 second edit including one ripple delete, one adjusted boundary, one continuity improvement, notes for three edits and a full playback review.

## Common errors and recovery
- Ripple delete shifts music out of sync: inspect track targeting and linked clips; undo, then lock or deliberately include affected tracks.
- Roll changes the wrong boundary: confirm the selected edit point and adjacent handles.
- Slip creates black frames: the source lacks sufficient unused handles; reduce the slip or choose another range.
- Slide creates a gap: inspect neighboring handles and snapping; undo and reassess.
- Continuity jump remains: compare action, direction and eyeline; use a cutaway only when it supports the story.

## Knowledge check
1. Which operation removes a gap and moves later clips?
2. Which operation moves the boundary but preserves total duration?
3. Which operation changes source frames inside a fixed clip?
4. What is a continuity error?
5. Why review at normal playback speed?

## Answer key
1. Ripple delete.
2. Roll edit.
3. Slip edit.
4. A mismatch disrupting the viewer's understanding of action, space or time.
5. Rhythm and perceived continuity are experienced in motion.

## Assessment rubric — 25 points
Trim operation selection 6; gap and sync management 5; continuity 6; pacing 4; notes and playback review 4. Pass: 20/25. An uncorrected major sync error requires revision.

## Quality-control checklist
- [ ] No unintended gaps.
- [ ] Dialogue and music remain synchronized.
- [ ] Action is understandable across cuts.
- [ ] No flash frames or unintended black frames.
- [ ] Duration matches the brief.
- [ ] Full edit reviewed at normal speed.
