# CT-01 Lesson 03 — Timeline Navigation and the First Assembly

- Stage: Beginner
- Practice time: 90–120 minutes
- Prerequisites: Lessons 01–02
- Tools: Any NLE with a timeline
- Assets: 3–6 short clips, one voice or natural-sound recording and licensed/owned music

## Learning outcomes
Identify the major NLE panels; create a sequence; place clips on a timeline; distinguish video and audio tracks; preview and save a first assembly.

## Key definitions
- Source viewer: displays a source clip before it is placed in the sequence.
- Program viewer: displays the current timeline output.
- Playhead: indicates the current timeline position.
- Track: horizontal lane holding video or audio clips.
- Clip: segment of media used in an edit.
- In/Out points: start and end boundaries of a selected source range.
- Insert edit: places material and shifts later content to make room.
- Overwrite edit: places material over an existing range, replacing it.
- Assembly: early arrangement of selected shots before detailed refinement.

## Why this matters
The timeline turns separate recordings into a sequence. Understanding track layers, playhead position and edit operations prevents accidental replacement and makes the edit predictable.

## Theory
The source viewer is for selecting material; the program viewer shows the edited sequence. Upper video tracks commonly cover lower tracks. Multiple audio tracks may play simultaneously, so dialogue and music need independent control.

Build a clear assembly before adding effects. First establish the story order and useful information.

## Procedure
1. Create a sequence using the project settings from Lesson 01.
2. Preview each source clip and identify its useful action or information.
3. Mark in/out points if supported.
4. Arrange shots in an order that establishes context, develops the idea and provides an ending.
5. Place dialogue or natural sound on an audio track aligned with the picture.
6. Place music on a separate track.
7. Play from the beginning and look for gaps, black frames, repeated shots or confusing order.
8. Save a checkpoint before major revisions.
9. Write one sentence describing what the assembly communicates.

## Worked example
Story: a handmade object is created. Shot A establishes the workspace; Shot B shows hands shaping the material; Shot C reveals the finished object. Arrange A → B → C. Use clean natural sound from B if it supports the scene. Add music only after the picture sequence makes sense.

## Dry run
A 20-second planning structure could be 0–4 seconds establishing context, 4–14 seconds showing the process and 14–20 seconds revealing the result. These are targets, not fixed rules; footage and story determine final timing.

## Guided practice
Create a 20–30 second assembly using at least three shots. Put voice or natural sound on a separate audio track. Note one moment where the story is unclear.

## Independent assignment
Create a 30–45 second rough assembly with five selected shots, separate dialogue/natural sound and music tracks, a saved checkpoint and a short edit note explaining shot order.

## Common errors and recovery
- Clip placed at wrong playhead position: undo, reposition and repeat.
- Upper track hides intended shot: inspect track order and visibility.
- Music covers dialogue: lower music for review and balance tracks independently.
- No clear opening: use a shot that establishes place, subject or question.
- Editing only to music beats: preserve understandable visual information and story logic.

## Knowledge check
1. What does the playhead indicate?
2. How does insert differ from overwrite?
3. Why separate dialogue and music tracks?
4. Source viewer versus program viewer?
5. What should an assembly establish before effects?

## Answer key
1. Current timeline position.
2. Insert shifts later content; overwrite replaces material in the target range.
3. Independent timing and level control.
4. Source viewer previews source media; program viewer previews the sequence.
5. Basic order, meaning and continuity.

## Assessment rubric — 25 points
Sequence and track setup 5; shot selection/story order 6; sound alignment 4; clear beginning/development/ending 5; checkpoint and edit notes 5. Pass: 20/25.

## Quality-control checklist
- [ ] Sequence plays start to finish.
- [ ] No unintended gaps or black frames.
- [ ] Shot order communicates a clear idea.
- [ ] Dialogue/natural sound is aligned.
- [ ] Music is independently controllable.
- [ ] Project checkpoint is saved.
