# CT-01 — Intermediate: Dialogue, Music and Basic Audio Mixing

**Course:** Video Editing  
**Pathway stage:** Intermediate

## Learning outcomes
Organize dialogue, ambience, effects and music; remove avoidable noise; use fades and clip gain; balance tracks; monitor peaks and loudness without relying on one meter.

## Prerequisites
Lessons 01–07 and source audio recorded with permission.

## Key definitions
- Gain: level adjustment applied to a signal.
- Clip gain: level adjustment applied to an individual clip before track processing.
- Fader: track-level control.
- Peak: highest instantaneous signal level.
- RMS: measure related to average signal energy over time.
- LUFS: loudness measurement weighted for perceived programme loudness.
- EQ: equalization, adjusting frequency balance.
- Compression: dynamic processing that reduces level differences above a threshold.
- Room tone: recording of the acoustic environment without foreground speech.
- Ducking: automatic or manual reduction of one audio source while another is prominent.

## Why it matters
Viewers may tolerate modest picture imperfections but quickly abandon content with unintelligible dialogue, harsh peaks or distracting music. Audio is part of the storytelling, not a final decoration.

## Theory
Separate dialogue, ambience, effects and music onto controllable tracks. Start with clip gain and edit cleanup before adding processors. Remove clicks with edits/fades; use noise reduction carefully because aggressive processing can create metallic artifacts. EQ and compression are corrective tools, not substitutes for good source recording.

There is no single loudness target suitable for every platform, territory and delivery specification. Follow the commissioning/platform requirement. Prevent digital clipping and inspect true peaks where a delivery spec requires it.

## Tools and alternatives
NLE audio mixer, waveform display, headphones, speakers and optional dedicated audio editor. Use a quiet listening environment; headphones alone can hide playback translation issues.

## Procedure
1. Separate dialogue, natural sound, ambience, effects and music.
2. Confirm synchronization and channel mapping.
3. Listen to dialogue alone and mark noise, clicks, clipping and distracting breaths.
4. Use clip gain to make dialogue phrases reasonably consistent before compression.
5. Apply short fades at edits to prevent clicks.
6. Add room tone or ambience where edits create unnatural silence.
7. Set dialogue as the intelligibility anchor.
8. Bring music up underneath dialogue; reduce it where words become difficult to understand.
9. Use EQ only to address identifiable issues; make small changes and compare bypassed/processed audio.
10. Use gentle compression only when dynamics need control; avoid pumping or audible artifacts.
11. Check peak meters and the specified loudness measurement; do not normalize blindly.
12. Listen on headphones and speakers at a comfortable level, then review the full programme.

## Worked example
A voice-over is clear but varies between phrases. Adjust clip gain to reduce large differences, then use light compression if needed. Place music beneath it and automate a small reduction during important words. Check that music remains audible without masking consonants.

## Dry run
Create three mixes: dialogue only, dialogue plus music, and dialogue plus music with automated ducking. Compare intelligibility and emotional tone. Record meter readings, but judge by listening as well.

## Guided exercise
Balance a 30-second voice clip with music. Use at least two fades and one volume automation move. Export a short test and listen on two playback systems.

## Independent assignment
Deliver a 60-second mix with separate dialogue, ambience/effects and music tracks; include a track map, processing notes, peak/loudness report and final listening checklist.

## Common errors and recovery
- Dialogue masked by music: lower music or automate ducking; do not simply boost the master.
- Clicks at edits: add short fades and inspect waveform boundaries.
- Noise reduction sounds watery: reduce processing or use room tone and manual repair.
- Compression pumps: reduce ratio/amount, adjust attack/release or bypass.
- Peaks exceed spec: identify the offending track and correct gain before limiting.
- Headphones sound good but speakers do not: test multiple systems and adjust translation issues.

## Knowledge check
1. Why adjust clip gain before compression?
2. What is room tone used for?
3. What does LUFS measure?
4. Why is there no universal loudness target?
5. What should you do when noise reduction creates artifacts?

## Answer key
1. To reduce large source-level differences before dynamic processing.
2. To maintain natural acoustic continuity beneath edits.
3. Perceived programme loudness.
4. Requirements vary by platform, territory and delivery specification.
5. Reduce or bypass the processing and use less destructive repair.

## Assessment rubric — 30 points
Dialogue intelligibility 8; track organization 5; edit cleanliness 4; music balance 5; technical measurement 4; listening report 4. Pass 24/30.

## Quality-control checklist
- [ ] Dialogue intelligible throughout.
- [ ] No unintended clicks, pops or clipping.
- [ ] Music does not mask speech.
- [ ] Fades and ambience sound natural.
- [ ] Peak/loudness checks match the delivery brief.
- [ ] Mix checked on more than one playback system.

## Further study
Study room acoustics, phase, stereo/mono compatibility, EQ, compression and loudness standards. Confirm target values from the actual delivery specification.
