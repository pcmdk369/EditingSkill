# CT-01 — Intermediate: Transitions, J-Cuts, L-Cuts and Visual Continuity

**Course:** Video Editing  
**Pathway stage:** Intermediate

## Learning outcomes
Select cuts and transitions based on story purpose; build J-cuts and L-cuts; control continuity and screen direction; diagnose transition problems.

## Prerequisites
Lessons 01–04 and a rough sequence with dialogue or natural sound.

## Key definitions
- Cut: immediate change from one shot to another.
- Dissolve: gradual blend between two shots.
- Wipe: one image replaces another through a moving boundary.
- J-cut: incoming audio begins before its picture appears.
- L-cut: outgoing audio continues after its picture has changed.
- Eyeline match: shot order respects where a subject appears to look.
- 180-degree rule: a continuity guideline that keeps camera positions on one side of an established action axis unless a motivated transition resets geography.
- Match cut: transition linking shots through visual, movement or conceptual similarity.

## Why it matters
Transitions shape time, space and attention. A transition should clarify a relationship or create a deliberate effect, not conceal weak shot selection.

## Theory
A straight cut is usually the least distracting transition and should be the default. Dissolves can suggest elapsed time, memory or a relationship between images; wipes are stylistic and should have a clear visual rationale. J/L cuts create audio continuity across picture edits and are essential to natural dialogue scenes.

The 180-degree rule is a guide, not an absolute law. Crossing the axis can work when the audience is shown the camera move, a neutral shot, or a new establishing view that reorients geography.

## Tools and alternatives
Use timeline audio/video track controls, transition handles, keyframes and markers. If an editor lacks dedicated J/L-cut tools, unlink or independently trim audio and video on a duplicate sequence.

## Procedure
1. Watch the assembly and mark every transition that attracts attention.
2. Ask what relationship each transition communicates: same continuous action, elapsed time, location change or thematic association.
3. Keep straight cuts where no special transition is needed.
4. Add a dissolve only when the change in time or meaning benefits from a gradual blend.
5. For dialogue, extend the incoming speaker's audio under the preceding shot to form a J-cut where appropriate.
6. Let the outgoing speaker's audio continue over the next shot to form an L-cut where appropriate.
7. Check eyelines, screen direction and the 180-degree axis.
8. If crossing the axis, add a motivated camera move, neutral shot or establishing shot.
9. Review transitions at normal speed and with audio; remove effects that distract.
10. Save a comparison version and note the narrative reason for each non-standard transition.

## Worked example
In an interview scene, show the listener reacting while the next speaker's first words begin. The audio lead introduces the next thought before the picture changes: a J-cut. Later, keep the speaker's final words over a cutaway of the subject being discussed: an L-cut.

## Dry run
Create three versions of the same edit: straight cut, 12-frame dissolve and J/L-cut. Compare which version best preserves time, place and dialogue flow. Durations are exercise values, not universal prescriptions.

## Guided exercise
Choose six edit points. Keep at least four as straight cuts; use a dissolve or J/L-cut only where it improves meaning. Write a one-sentence rationale for each effect.

## Independent assignment
Edit a 45–60 second two-person conversation with at least two J/L cuts, consistent eyelines and an establishing shot. Submit a clean version and a version with intentional transition choices.

## Common errors and recovery
- Dissolve produces ghosting during fast movement: shorten it or use a cut.
- Audio pop at an edit: add a short appropriate fade and inspect waveform boundaries.
- Dialogue sounds detached from picture: check sync and the J/L-cut overlap.
- Screen direction reverses unexpectedly: review the axis and insert a reorientation shot if needed.
- Every cut has an effect: remove decorative transitions and restore straight cuts.

## Knowledge check
1. What story purpose can a dissolve serve?
2. Define J-cut and L-cut.
3. What does the 180-degree guideline protect?
4. When can crossing the axis be understandable?
5. Why should a straight cut remain the default?

## Answer key
1. Suggest elapsed time, memory or a deliberate relationship between images.
2. J: incoming audio precedes its picture; L: outgoing audio continues over the next picture.
3. Consistent screen geography and viewer orientation.
4. With a motivated move, neutral shot or establishing reorientation.
5. It is direct and usually least distracting.

## Assessment rubric — 25 points
Transition rationale 5; J/L-cut execution 8; eyeline and screen direction 6; audio continuity 4; comparison notes 2. Pass 20/25.

## Quality-control checklist
- [ ] Transitions have a clear purpose.
- [ ] Dialogue remains synchronized.
- [ ] No unintended audio gaps or pops.
- [ ] Eyelines and screen direction are coherent.
- [ ] Full sequence reviewed at normal speed.

## Further study
Study montage, match cuts, parallel editing and scene geography. Transition behavior and frame-based duration controls vary by editor version.
