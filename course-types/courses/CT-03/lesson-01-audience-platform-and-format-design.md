# CT-03 Lesson 01 — Audience, Platform and Format Design

- **Course:** CT-03 Short-Form & Social Video Editing
- **Level:** Foundation → Beginner
- **Estimated time:** 90–120 minutes
- **Prerequisites:** Basic file management; no editing software experience required
- **Software:** Any editor that supports a custom sequence/canvas; software-neutral
- **Version note:** Platform specifications and upload limits change. Confirm current requirements in the destination platform's official creator documentation before final export.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of this lesson, the learner can:
1. Convert a short-form video brief into a measurable audience and message statement.
2. Distinguish aspect ratio, frame size, frame rate, duration and safe area.
3. Select a sequence format from delivery requirements rather than habit.
4. Create a reusable specification sheet for a vertical edit.

## Key definitions

- **Short-form video:** A concise moving-image work designed for rapid viewing and a specific communication goal; duration is determined by brief and platform, not by one universal definition.
- **Aspect ratio:** Width-to-height relationship, such as 9:16 or 16:9.
- **Frame size/resolution:** Pixel dimensions of each video frame, for example 1080 × 1920.
- **Frame rate:** Number of frames displayed per second, expressed as fps.
- **Safe area:** Region in which essential text and graphics remain clear of interface overlays and edge cropping.
- **Hook:** Opening image, action, question or statement that gives the audience a reason to continue.
- **Call to action (CTA):** A clear next step requested from the viewer.
- **Deliverable specification:** Written record of required output properties and acceptance criteria.

## Purpose and theory

A technically correct export can still fail if it addresses the wrong viewer or hides its message beneath platform interface elements. Design begins with communication: who should watch, what they should understand or feel, and what they should do next. Only then should the editor choose framing and export settings.

A vertical 9:16 composition is common for phone-first feeds, but it is not automatically correct for every brief. A square, landscape or multi-format campaign may be required. Do not crop a horizontal master blindly: important subjects, captions, product details and graphics may be outside the new frame.

## Tools and alternatives

Use a brief, notes app or spreadsheet to record the specification. Any editor with custom sequence dimensions can create the sequence. If the editor lacks safe-area guides, create a temporary overlay guide on a separate track and disable it before export.

## Procedure

1. Read the brief and underline the audience, message, platform, duration, language and CTA.
2. Write one audience sentence: “This video is for [audience] who need/want [need].”
3. Write one message sentence: “After watching, the viewer should understand [single message].”
4. Identify the evidence needed to make the message credible: demonstration, interview, product detail, result, comparison or source citation.
5. Choose the destination aspect ratio from the brief. For a vertical phone-first deliverable, 9:16 is a common starting point; confirm the platform's current requirements.
6. Choose frame dimensions that meet the brief and source quality. Do not upscale low-resolution footage and assume detail has been restored.
7. Choose a frame rate compatible with source footage and intended motion. Avoid unnecessary frame-rate conversion.
8. Record duration as a target range, not an arbitrary hard rule. Keep the message complete.
9. Identify interface-obscured regions. Place essential text away from edges and likely controls; verify with current platform guides.
10. Define caption requirements: burned-in captions, sidecar subtitle file, or both.
11. Define audio requirements, language, accessibility needs, file naming and delivery formats.
12. Create a one-page specification before opening the timeline.

## Worked example

**Brief:** A maker wants a 20–30 second vertical video showing a handmade leather wallet and directing interested viewers to a product page.

- Audience: people comparing durable handmade accessories.
- Message: show material, craftsmanship and one practical feature.
- Evidence: close-up stitching, edge finishing and wallet opening.
- Opening: a clear close-up plus a concise question.
- Middle: three proof shots, each with one short caption.
- Ending: finished product and CTA.
- Format: vertical 9:16, dimensions confirmed against the chosen editor and platform.
- Captions: readable, high-contrast, inside safe composition.
- Acceptance: message understandable without audio; no essential detail hidden by UI.

## Dry run

A source clip is 1920 × 1080. The destination is 9:16. Calculate the crop width if the full 1080-pixel height is retained:

Width = 1080 × 9 ÷ 16 = 607.5 pixels.

The crop therefore retains only about 608 pixels of the original 1920-pixel width. This demonstrates why reframing must be deliberate: the subject may be cut off. Prefer a vertical source, repositionable subject, or designed multi-format composition where available.

## Guided practice

Choose a learner-created or properly licensed source clip. Write a specification sheet containing audience, message, platform, aspect ratio, dimensions, frame rate, duration, captions, audio, CTA and acceptance criteria. Ask a peer to identify any ambiguous requirement.

## Independent assignment

Prepare specifications for two versions of the same message: a vertical social cut and a landscape website cut. Explain what must be recomposed rather than simply cropped.

## Common errors and recovery

- **Choosing dimensions before reading the brief:** return to the deliverable requirements and document the reason for each setting.
- **Assuming every platform uses identical safe areas:** check current platform documentation and preview the actual upload interface.
- **Putting all information on screen:** prioritize one message and move supporting detail to the caption or destination page.
- **Cropping the center automatically:** inspect subject position and reframe shot by shot.
- **Using tiny captions to fit everything:** shorten the copy and improve hierarchy rather than shrinking text.

## Knowledge check

1. What is the difference between aspect ratio and resolution?
2. Why is a safe area needed?
3. Does 9:16 automatically suit every social-video brief?
4. What should be decided before selecting export settings?
5. Why can a landscape-to-vertical crop remove important information?

### Answer guidance

1. Aspect ratio is the proportional relationship; resolution is the pixel dimensions.
2. Platform controls, cropping and device presentation can obscure edge content.
3. No; follow the actual brief and destination.
4. Audience, message, platform, content and deliverable requirements.
5. A vertical crop retains a narrow portion of the landscape width.
