# CT-03 Lesson 03 — Vertical Reframing, Captions and Safe Composition

- **Course:** CT-03 Short-Form & Social Video Editing
- **Level:** Intermediate
- **Estimated time:** 120–150 minutes
- **Prerequisites:** Lessons 01–02; a sequence containing dialogue or a clearly visible subject
- **Software:** Timeline editor with crop/transform controls and text layers; caption editor or subtitle tool

## Learning outcomes

The learner can reframe mixed-orientation footage for a vertical sequence, maintain subject continuity, design readable captions, and inspect the final composition on a phone-sized preview.

## Terminology

- **Reframing:** Changing the visible composition to suit a new frame.
- **Tracking:** Following a moving subject or point so framing or an effect remains aligned.
- **Keyframe:** A stored parameter value at a specific timeline position; intermediate values are calculated by interpolation.
- **Subtitle/caption:** Timed text representing speech or relevant audio information. Platform conventions and accessibility requirements may differ.
- **Burned-in caption:** Text rendered into the image.
- **Sidecar subtitle:** A separate timed text file accompanying the video.
- **Contrast:** Difference in luminance or color that helps an element remain distinguishable.
- **Line break:** Intentional division of caption text to support reading.

## Composition principles

A vertical frame has less horizontal space than a landscape frame. Reframing is therefore an editorial decision, not just a technical crop. Keep the subject's important action visible, preserve enough context to understand the scene, and avoid placing text over faces, hands, product details or critical motion.

Captions should be synchronized, accurate and readable. Do not rely on color alone to distinguish speakers. Use adequate contrast, consistent typography and line lengths that can be read at normal playback speed. Avoid placing essential words beneath platform controls.

## Procedure: vertical reframing

1. Duplicate or create a new sequence; preserve the landscape master.
2. Set the sequence dimensions to the approved vertical specification.
3. Place one source clip and inspect the full frame.
4. Identify the subject's focal point and the action that must remain visible.
5. Adjust scale and position; avoid excessive enlargement that reveals pixelation.
6. If the subject moves, use position keyframes or a tracking feature where available.
7. Review the entire clip for headroom, hand movement and object visibility.
8. Check cuts between adjacent shots so the subject does not jump unpredictably across the frame.
9. Use a wider shot or a designed split-screen when a single vertical crop cannot preserve necessary context.
10. Repeat for every shot; do not assume one crop setting works for all clips.

## Procedure: captions

1. Create or obtain a transcript from the actual audio.
2. Correct names, technical terms, punctuation and speaker attribution.
3. Divide text into readable phrases rather than displaying a full paragraph.
4. Synchronize each phrase to the audible speech; do not reveal words substantially before they are spoken unless the creative format clearly requires it.
5. Keep captions away from faces, important objects and platform interface zones.
6. Use consistent font, weight, size, background treatment and speaker labels.
7. Check line breaks and reading time at normal speed.
8. Review the video muted and compare every caption against the source audio.
9. Export burned-in captions, a sidecar file or both according to the delivery brief.
10. If captions are translated, require a fluent human review for meaning, names, timing and cultural context.

## Worked example

A horizontal interview shot shows a speaker on the left and a demonstration object on the right. A center crop would remove one of them. Options include:
- keyframing between the speaker and object when the edit allows;
- using a vertical split composition;
- cutting between a speaker close-up and a separate object detail;
- using a wider vertical-safe source recording.

Choose the option that preserves the meaning and avoids distracting motion.

## Dry run: caption timing

A spoken phrase lasts 2.4 seconds and contains eight words. At normal speed, the phrase may be readable as one caption if the words and line breaks are simple. If the phrase contains a long technical term or a proper name, split it into two timed units. Check the actual reading experience; word count alone cannot guarantee readability.

## Guided practice

Reframe three landscape clips into a vertical sequence. One clip should contain a moving subject. Add accurate captions to a 15–20 second dialogue section. Export a review copy and inspect it on a phone display.

## Independent assignment

Deliver:
- vertical sequence and editable project;
- caption transcript or subtitle file;
- a 10–20 second preview with captions;
- a short note explaining reframing decisions and any source limitations.

## Common errors and recovery

- **Subject leaves frame:** add keyframes, use a wider shot or cut to another angle.
- **Excessive zoom:** return to source resolution or use a different composition.
- **Captions cover the subject:** reposition text or change shot selection.
- **Caption timing drifts:** compare against waveform and listen to the full phrase.
- **Poor contrast:** add a restrained background/outline and test on bright and dark footage.
- **Overlong caption blocks:** divide by natural phrase boundaries and re-time.
- **Translation changes meaning:** restore the original meaning and obtain competent language review.

## Knowledge check

1. Why should the landscape master be preserved?
2. What is a keyframe used for in reframing?
3. What is the difference between burned-in and sidecar captions?
4. Why must captions be checked against the source audio?
5. What should be done when a vertical crop cannot show both the speaker and the object?

### Answer guidance

1. It preserves the original composition and supports future versions.
2. It changes a parameter over time, such as position or scale.
3. Burned-in text is part of the image; sidecar text is a separate timed file.
4. Automatic transcription can mishear words, names and technical terms.
5. Use an alternate angle, keyframed view, split composition or wider source.

Subject visibility 6; motion continuity 4; caption accuracy 6; readability/accessibility 5; export and project organization 4. Suggested pass: 20/25.

## Quality-control checklist

- [ ] Original master remains unchanged.
- [ ] Every shot has an intentional vertical crop.
- [ ] Moving subjects remain visible.
- [ ] No important action is hidden by text.
- [ ] Captions match the source audio.
- [ ] Text is readable on a phone-sized preview.
- [ ] Export and editable project are both delivered.
