# CT-03 Capstone — Three-Format Social Campaign

- Course: CT-03 — Short-Form & Social Video Editing
- Level: Master / Professional Practice
- Estimated time: 8–12 hours
- Status: Capstone brief authored; learner execution and review not yet evidenced

## 1. Scenario
A small business or creator needs one coherent campaign built from a single authorized source recording. Produce three distinct short-form edits for three delivery contexts. Each version must preserve factual accuracy while adapting framing, pace and CTA to its destination.

## 2. Required outputs
1. **Vertical edit:** phone-first 9:16 composition.
2. **Square edit:** 1:1 composition for a square feed or catalog context.
3. **Landscape edit:** 16:9 composition for website or horizontal placement.

The brief determines exact pixel dimensions, frame rate, duration, codec and platform restrictions. Do not assume a universal export preset.

## 3. Source and rights
Use original learner footage or properly licensed assets. Record releases/consent for identifiable people, music licenses, stock terms and third-party graphics. Do not use synthetic voice or likeness without explicit permission and applicable disclosure.

## 4. Required planning documents
- audience/message brief;
- platform and export specification;
- storyboard or shot map for each version;
- selects log with source timecodes;
- asset/rights manifest;
- caption transcript and language notes;
- schedule and version/change log.

## 5. Editorial requirements
Each edit must have a truthful opening, comprehensible story progression, evidence supporting the central message and an appropriate ending. The three edits may share a campaign identity but must not be mere automatic crops. Reframe every shot and check captions, titles and interface-safe placement independently.

## 6. Technical and accessibility requirements
- match each delivery specification;
- preserve dialogue intelligibility;
- provide accurate captions/subtitles where required;
- use readable text and adequate contrast;
- inspect complete exports, not only the editing timeline;
- keep an editable project and all required dependencies;
- document any unsupported requirement or source limitation.

## 7. Submission manifest
```text
capstone/
  brief/
  source-and-rights/
  project/
  exports/
    vertical/
    square/
    landscape/
  captions/
  qc/
  delivery/
  reflection/
```

Submit three final exports, editable project, linked assets, specification sheet, rights log, captions, QC report, delivery manifest and a 500–800 word reflection.

## 8. Rubric — 100 points
| Criterion | Points |
|---|---:|
| Brief interpretation and story clarity | 15 |
| Truthful hook, evidence and payoff | 15 |
| Three independent compositions | 20 |
| Pacing and audience fit | 10 |
| Caption/audio/accessibility quality | 10 |
| Technical export and full playback QC | 10 |
| Rights, consent and provenance | 10 |
| Project organization, handoff and reflection | 10 |
| **Total** | **100** |

Pass threshold: 80/100. Rights/consent, factual integrity, master/source preservation and file playability are mandatory gates; failure requires correction regardless of total score.

## 9. Review process
Reviewer checks each export against its own specification, verifies source and rights documentation, watches every file, and records defects with timecodes. Revisions must be versioned. A capstone is validated only after evidence of actual exports and review is submitted.

## 10. Reflection prompts
- Which editorial choices changed between formats and why?
- Which source moments were essential evidence?
- What was lost or gained in each adaptation?
- What defects were found during export QC?
- Which tools or automation steps were used, and what required human review?
- What would be changed in a second production cycle?
