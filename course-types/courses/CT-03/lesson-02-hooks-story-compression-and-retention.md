# CT-03 Lesson 02 — Hooks, Story Compression and Retention

- **Course:** CT-03 Short-Form & Social Video Editing
- **Level:** Beginner → Intermediate
- **Estimated time:** 120 minutes
- **Prerequisites:** CT-03 Lesson 01; a short source recording with a clear subject or process
- **Software:** Any timeline editor with trimming, markers and text layers

## Learning outcomes

The learner can identify a truthful hook, reduce a larger story to one central idea, arrange evidence in a comprehensible order, and revise pacing without removing necessary context.

## Definitions

- **Hook:** Opening element that establishes curiosity, relevance or immediate value.
- **Story compression:** Removing repetition and low-value material while preserving meaning, cause and effect.
- **Information density:** Amount of useful information communicated in a given period.
- **Retention:** The extent to which viewers continue watching; it is an observed outcome, not a guarantee created by one editing trick.
- **Pattern interrupt:** A meaningful change in image, sound, framing or information that renews attention.
- **Payoff:** The answer, result or emotional resolution promised by the opening.
- **Cold open:** Beginning with a compelling moment before background or title information.

## Why this matters

Short-form editing has limited time, but speed is not the same as clarity. A sequence that cuts every half-second can be exhausting and confusing. Strong compression preserves the viewer's ability to understand the subject while removing delays and repeated points.

A hook must be supported by the content. Do not promise a result the footage cannot demonstrate, misrepresent a quote, or create false urgency. The edit should earn the viewer's attention rather than deceive them.

## Story models

Choose a structure appropriate to the material:

1. **Problem → process → result:** useful for tutorials and demonstrations.
2. **Question → evidence → answer:** useful for explainers and product features.
3. **Before → change → after:** useful for transformations, provided the comparison is fair.
4. **Claim → proof → limitation:** useful for factual or commercial content.
5. **Moment → context → meaning:** useful for interviews and human stories.

These are planning aids, not mandatory formulas.

## Procedure

1. Watch all source material once without editing.
2. Write the central message in one sentence.
3. Identify the strongest truthful visual or spoken moment.
4. Draft three possible openings: a question, a result-first image and a direct statement.
5. Reject any opening that exaggerates, removes essential context or makes an unsupported claim.
6. Build a selects list with source filename, in/out timecode and purpose.
7. Group selects into opening, evidence/process, complication or contrast, and payoff.
8. Remove repeated explanations and actions that do not advance the message.
9. Preserve transitions in logic: show enough action for viewers to understand what changed.
10. Use a pattern interrupt only when it adds information, emphasis or rhythm.
11. Review the edit with sound off to test visual comprehension.
12. Review audio-only where dialogue carries the message; identify unclear or missing context.
13. Ask a viewer unfamiliar with the subject to summarize the message.
14. Revise based on confusion, not merely on a preference for faster cuts.

## Worked example

**Raw material:** 90 seconds of a cook preparing a dish. The intended edit is approximately 25 seconds.

- Opening: finished dish and a truthful question about the key technique.
- Evidence: ingredient preparation and the critical cooking action.
- Detail: close-up that proves texture or doneness.
- Payoff: finished dish, serving and concise CTA.

Remove repeated stirring and unrelated setup. Keep the action that explains the technique. If a time jump is used, make it clear through a cutaway, caption or other honest cue.

## Dry run: six-shot compression

Source selects have durations 8, 12, 6, 10, 9 and 15 seconds. Total = 60 seconds. The target is 30 seconds.

Do not halve every shot automatically. First label each shot's function. A possible rough allocation is 3 seconds for the hook, 6 seconds for setup, 12 seconds for essential process/evidence, 5 seconds for result and 4 seconds for CTA. The allocation is a starting hypothesis; revise after reviewing clarity and source action.

## Guided practice

Create a 20–30 second edit from at least six source moments. Produce two different openings while keeping the body identical. Ask three viewers which opening communicates the subject most clearly and why. Record responses; do not treat a tiny sample as statistically representative.

## Independent assignment

Create a 30-second and a 45-second version from the same footage. Submit the selects log, story statement and a short explanation of what information was removed and why.

## Common errors and recovery

- **Misleading hook:** replace it with a claim directly supported by footage.
- **Context removed:** restore a setup shot, qualifier or source statement.
- **Cuts feel frantic:** lengthen shots where the viewer needs time to inspect or understand.
- **Repetitive middle:** remove duplicate evidence and retain the strongest example.
- **Payoff arrives too late:** move the key result earlier, then show how it was achieved.
- **Unclear time jump:** add an honest transition or concise label.

## Knowledge check

1. What makes a hook truthful?
2. What is information density?
3. Name two story structures suitable for short-form edits.
4. Why should an edit be reviewed without sound?
5. Does retention prove that a video is accurate or useful?

### Answer guidance

1. It creates interest without promising unsupported information.
2. The useful information communicated over time.
3. Any two listed structures.
4. It reveals whether the visual sequence communicates without dialogue or music.
5. No; retention is one audience measure and does not establish accuracy or value.

## Assessment rubric — 25 points

Story statement 4; truthful hook 5; evidence sequence 6; compression and pacing 5; context and ethical accuracy 3; reflection 2. Suggested pass: 20/25.

## Quality-control checklist

- [ ] Opening is relevant and supported by the material.
- [ ] One central message is identifiable.
- [ ] Evidence appears in a logical order.
- [ ] Necessary context and qualifiers remain.
- [ ] Every cut serves information, emotion or rhythm.
- [ ] Payoff resolves the opening promise.
- [ ] Review notes are recorded.
