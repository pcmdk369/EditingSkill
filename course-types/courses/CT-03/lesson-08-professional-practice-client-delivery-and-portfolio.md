# CT-03 Lesson 08 — Professional Practice, Client Delivery and Portfolio

- Course: CT-03 — Short-Form & Social Video Editing
- Level: Professional Practice
- Estimated time: 150–180 minutes
- Prerequisites: Lessons 01–07; completed multi-format capstone
- Tools/environment: Project management tool or spreadsheet, editor, delivery storage and portfolio platform
- Version/review date: Tool-neutral

## Learning outcomes
Learners can scope a client brief, estimate work and revisions, prepare a professional handoff, protect rights and privacy, and present portfolio work with accurate attribution.

## Key definitions
| Term | Definition | Context |
|---|---|---|
| Scope | Agreed work, deliverables and exclusions | Three short-form edits |
| Acceptance criteria | Observable conditions for approval | Runtime and format |
| Revision round | Defined cycle of consolidated feedback | One review pass |
| Change request | Proposed work outside agreed scope | Additional language version |
| Handoff | Transfer of final files, project dependencies and documentation | Client delivery |
| Portfolio case study | Evidence-led presentation of process and outcome | Brief, role, deliverables |

## Purpose and professional relevance
Professional editing includes communication, scope control, rights management and reliable delivery. A polished export is insufficient if the client cannot identify the approved file, reopen the project or verify asset permissions. Portfolio claims must accurately represent the learner's contribution and must not reveal confidential work.

## Procedure
1. Clarify audience, objective, deliverables, formats, runtime, languages and deadline.
2. Define exclusions, review rounds, feedback owner and approval authority.
3. Inventory assets and record licenses, releases, music rights and third-party restrictions.
4. Estimate ingest, edit, caption, versioning, QC and revision time separately.
5. Confirm project milestones and delivery acceptance criteria in writing.
6. Use a change request for scope additions or factual changes.
7. Package final exports with clear filenames and a delivery manifest.
8. Include editable project and media dependencies when agreed.
9. Record software/version, fonts/plugins and any linked assets needed to reopen.
10. Confirm client receipt and archive according to agreed retention policy.
11. Obtain permission before using client work in a portfolio.
12. Present case studies with role, constraints, decisions, outputs and verifiable results; distinguish personal contribution from team work.

## Worked example
A client orders two vertical edits and one subtitle file, one revision round and delivery by a specified date. A request for an additional language version is recorded as a change request, not silently added. The handoff includes two approved exports, subtitle file, manifest and agreed project package.

## Dry run
The client says “send the final one” but three versions exist. Do not guess. Reply with filenames and a preview/contact sheet or ask the client to identify the approved version. Once confirmed, mark it approved in the manifest.

## Guided practice
Write a one-page scope and acceptance checklist for a hypothetical creator campaign. Build a delivery folder and manifest from your capstone.

## Independent assignment
Submit client brief, scope, estimate assumptions, rights log, revision record, delivery manifest, archive note and a portfolio case study with confidential details removed.

## Common errors and recovery
| Symptom | Likely cause | Diagnosis | Recovery |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scope dispute | Deliverables unclear | Compare brief and requests | Document scope and change request |
| Client cannot reopen project | Dependencies missing | Test package on clean location | Collect linked assets and document versions |
| Portfolio breach | No publication permission | Check agreement | Remove work until authorized |
| Wrong final sent | Ambiguous filenames | Audit manifest | Confirm approval and resend correct file |
| Rights unclear | Asset source not logged | Audit media | Replace asset or obtain license/release |

## Knowledge check
1. What are acceptance criteria?
2. How should out-of-scope work be handled?
3. Why is a rights log important?
4. What belongs in a handoff?
5. Why is portfolio permission separate from delivery permission?

## Answer guidance
1. Measurable conditions that define acceptable delivery.
2. Record a change request and obtain agreement before proceeding.
3. It documents lawful use and reduces delivery/publication risk.
4. Approved exports, manifest, project/dependencies if agreed, versions and notes.
5. Permission to deliver a client asset does not necessarily authorize public display.

## Assessment rubric — 30 points
Scope and client communication 6; rights/privacy 6; delivery package 7; documentation 5; portfolio accuracy 6. Pass: 24/30; rights and confidentiality are mandatory.
