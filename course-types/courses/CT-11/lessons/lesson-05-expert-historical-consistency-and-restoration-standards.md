# CT-11 Lesson 05 — Historical Consistency and Restoration Standards

- Course: CT-11 — Photo Restoration & Manipulation
- Level: Expert
- Estimated time: 120–180 minutes
- Prerequisites: Previous CT-11 lessons and authorized source images
- Tools/environment: Raster editor, optional scanner/RAW processor and secure storage; record app/version and scan properties
- Version/review date: Tool-neutral; archival and privacy requirements depend on collection context

## Learning outcomes
Evaluate restoration choices against historical context, preserve evidence and maintain consistent output across a collection.

## Key definitions
- **Restoration:** Repairing damage while preserving the source's historical/visual meaning.
- **Provenance:** Documented origin and history of an image.
- **Clone/heal:** Pixel-copy and texture-blending repair methods.
- **Reconstruction:** Rebuilding missing content from evidence or interpretation.
- **Colorization:** Adding color to an originally monochrome image.
- **Preservation master:** High-quality retained version for long-term use.
- **Access copy:** Derivative optimized for viewing or distribution.
- **Intervention log:** Record of restoration operations and decisions.

## Purpose and theory
Restoration standards depend on archival, family, museum, editorial or commercial context. Some projects prioritize faithful conservation and minimal intervention; others permit interpretive presentation. Maintain the untouched source and a restoration log so future reviewers can distinguish original from reconstructed material.

## Tools, requirements and alternatives
Use a capable raster editor and a scanner/RAW workflow as needed. Preserve originals, create working copies and store layered masters. Use only owned or authorized images. Keep sensitive family/archival material secure.

## Procedure
1. Identify collection context and preservation objective. 2. Review dates, provenance and known reference material. 3. Define intervention levels: cleaning, repair, tonal correction, reconstruction, colorization. 4. Apply consistent treatment across a series. 5. Preserve source scan and layered master. 6. Document uncertainty and confidence. 7. Obtain curator/client review where applicable. 8. Export preservation and access copies separately.

## Worked example
A family archive includes multiple prints from the same era. The editor standardizes scan and dust cleanup but avoids imposing a modern color grade across all images.

## Dry run
One image is colorized while others remain original monochrome. Label versions and explain the interpretive method.

## Guided practice
Create a restoration standard for a five-image historical set.

## Independent assignment
Apply the method to a second image with a different damage type. Submit source, restored version, editable project, provenance and intervention log.

## Common errors and recovery
| Symptom | Likely cause | Diagnosis | Recovery |
|---|---|---|---|
| Submit standard, five restored files, source archive and intervention log. | Restoration issue | Compare to original and reference evidence | Revert unsupported edit and document |

## Knowledge check
1. Why must the original scan be preserved?
2. How do clone and healing tools differ?
3. What is the risk of reconstructing missing unique details?
4. Why should colorization be distinguished from restoration?
5. What belongs in an intervention log?

## Answer guidance
1. It preserves evidence and enables future re-evaluation.
2. Clone copies pixels; healing blends sampled texture/color with surroundings.
3. It can invent historical details and mislead viewers.
4. Colorization adds interpretive color not present in a monochrome source.
5. Operations, tool/version, source references, uncertainty and approvals.

## Assessment rubric — 20 points
| Criterion | Points | Evidence |
|---|---:|---|
| Source preservation/provenance | 4 | Original and source log |
| Repair quality | 5 | Before/after at full size |
| Historical/factual integrity | 5 | Reference and uncertainty record |
| Non-destructive project | 3 | Layered file |

Pass: 16/20. Fabricated historical evidence or unauthorized use requires remediation.

## Quality-control checklist
- [ ] Untouched original preserved.
- [ ] Provenance and rights recorded.
- [ ] Repairs are evidence-based.
- [ ] Reconstructed areas are documented.
- [ ] AI-generated detail is not misrepresented.
- [ ] Preservation and access copies are distinguished.
- [ ] Archive and disclosure records complete.
