# CT-09 — Portrait & Beauty Retouching Assessment

**Total:** 50 points | **Suggested time:** 45–60 minutes

## Part A — Multiple choice (10 × 2 = 20)

1. Retouch boundaries should be agreed: A. After publication B. Before editing C. Never D. Only after export.
2. Healing generally: A. Blends sampled texture with surroundings B. Copies exact pixels only C. Deletes source D. Changes frame rate.
3. Cloning generally: A. Copies pixels from a source area B. Corrects audio C. Adds captions D. Grants consent.
4. Natural skin retouch should preserve: A. Texture and identity B. Only blur C. All blemishes always D. Artificial symmetry.
5. Dodge and burn: A. Brighten/darken locally B. Change copyright C. Increase resolution D. Export video.
6. A halo around hair often indicates: A. Mask/edge issue B. Correct skin tone C. Music sync D. Metadata.
7. A portrait AI service should be checked for: A. Privacy/retention/consent B. Only font choice C. Aspect ratio only D. Export filename.
8. A client may automatically publish portraits in a portfolio. A. True B. False.
9. Batch retouch should: A. Be reviewed per subject B. Apply same skin tone to all C. Ignore lighting D. Replace subject approval.
10. Sensitive metadata should be: A. Considered before delivery B. Always public C. Never checked D. Invented.

## Part B — Short answer (5 × 3 = 15)

11. Distinguish temporary blemish from defining feature.
12. Name three checks for natural skin retouch.
13. Why should skin tone not be forced to one preset?
14. What consent/privacy questions apply to AI portrait enhancement?
15. What is included in a professional portrait handoff?

## Part C — Applied case (15)

16. A retouch makes skin plastic and changes the subject's eye shape. Describe a safe correction and review workflow. (8)
17. A client approves delivery but refuses portfolio publication. What should the editor do with public case-study materials? (7)

## Answer key
1 B; 2 A; 3 A; 4 A; 5 A; 6 A; 7 A; 8 B; 9 A; 10 A.
11. Temporary blemish is short-lived; defining feature contributes to identity/appearance and should not be removed without explicit scope/consent. (3)
12. Texture, natural transitions, skin/neck consistency, facial identity, output-size review. (3)
13. Lighting and complexion differ; uniform presets can distort skin tone. (3)
14. Informed consent, external processing, retention/training, biometric sensitivity, permitted use. (3)
15. Approved exports, source/project if agreed, consent/scope, manifest, privacy/metadata notes and QC. (3)
16. Restore original eye detail, reduce smoothing, use non-destructive layers, compare to source, obtain subject/client approval and log changes. (8)
17. Do not publish identifiable images or protected details. Use only a permitted anonymized description if contract/privacy allows, otherwise keep case study private. (7)

## Performance bands
45–50 exemplary; 40–44 competent; 35–39 developing; below 35 remediation. Consent/privacy breaches require correction.
