# CT-09 Lesson 07 — AI-Assisted Portrait Retouching, Consent and Identity

- Course: CT-09 — Portrait & Beauty Retouching
- Level: AI & Automation
- Estimated time: 120–180 minutes
- Prerequisites: Previous CT-09 lessons and authorized portrait assets
- Tools/environment: Raster editor/RAW processor; record app/version, OS and color profile
- Version/review date: Tool-neutral; privacy and consent obligations depend on jurisdiction and context

## Learning outcomes
Evaluate AI skin cleanup and enhancement while protecting biometric/identity-sensitive images, consent, privacy and source fidelity.

## Key definitions
- **Retouching:** Editing an image to correct or alter appearance within an agreed scope.
- **Healing:** Repair method that blends sampled texture/color into an area.
- **Cloning:** Copying pixels from one region to another.
- **Dodge and burn:** Local brightening and darkening.
- **Frequency separation:** Technique separating broad tone/color from fine texture for independent adjustment.
- **Mask:** Non-destructive visibility control.
- **Subject approval:** Review by the person depicted or authorized representative where agreed.

## Purpose and theory
Portrait images can be sensitive personal data. External processing may create privacy, retention and biometric risks. AI enhancement can alter facial features, invent details or change skin texture. Use only authorized tools, review service terms and keep a human reviewer accountable.

## Tools, requirements and alternatives
Use a capable raster editor and RAW processor where needed. Preserve originals, use separate layers and verify software version. Obtain permission for source images and follow the subject's agreed retouch scope. Treat personal portrait images securely.

## Procedure
1. Confirm subject consent and image-use scope. 2. Assess sensitivity and external processing risk. 3. Review provider retention/training terms. 4. Work on a duplicate source. 5. Use bounded cleanup rather than identity alteration. 6. Compare facial details and skin tone to source. 7. Log tool/model/version and changes. 8. Obtain subject/client approval and disclose material changes where needed.

## Worked example
AI removes a temporary blemish but also changes eye shape. Restore original eye detail and use only approved localized cleanup.

## Dry run
Service requests face enrollment for an enhancement workflow. Do not proceed without explicit informed consent and privacy/legal review.

## Guided practice
Compare manual and AI-assisted cleanup on a non-sensitive learner-created portrait.

## Independent assignment
Repeat the workflow with a second portrait under different lighting. Submit editable source, before/after proof, scope notes and QC evidence.

## Common errors and recovery
| Symptom | Likely cause | Diagnosis | Recovery |
|---|---|---|---|
| Submit risk assessment, provenance log, comparison and approval. | Retouch workflow issue | Inspect original, scope and output size | Restore natural detail and retest |

## Knowledge check
1. Why should retouch scope be agreed before editing?
2. How do healing and cloning differ?
3. Why is skin texture important?
4. What risks arise when portraits are uploaded to AI services?
5. Why is portfolio permission separate from delivery?

## Answer guidance
1. It protects subject intent, trust and project boundaries.
2. Healing blends with surroundings; cloning copies pixels.
3. It preserves natural appearance and avoids artificial smoothing.
4. Privacy, retention, biometric/identity sensitivity and unauthorized processing.
5. Client delivery does not automatically authorize public display.

## Quality-control checklist
- [ ] Subject consent and scope documented.
- [ ] Original image preserved.
- [ ] Skin texture and identity features remain natural.
- [ ] Skin tone and lighting are consistent.
- [ ] AI use is disclosed/reviewed where required.
- [ ] Sensitive metadata and files handled appropriately.
- [ ] Final exports and portfolio permissions checked.
