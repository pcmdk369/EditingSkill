# CT-14 Lesson 03 — Voice Cleanup, EQ, Compression and De-Essing

- Course: CT-14 — Dialogue, Voice & Podcast Editing
- Level: Intermediate
- Estimated time: 120–180 minutes
- Prerequisites: Previous CT-14 lessons and authorized speech recordings
- Tools/environment: DAW/audio editor, headphones, microphone and transcript tool; record OS/app/version
- Version/review date: Tool-neutral; publisher specs and privacy requirements must be verified

## Learning outcomes
Improve speech intelligibility with controlled noise cleanup, EQ, compression and sibilance reduction.

## Key definitions
- **Room tone:** Background sound of a recording space without foreground speech.
- **Plosive:** Burst of air causing a low-frequency pop in speech.
- **Sibilance:** High-frequency energy in sounds such as “s” and “sh”.
- **De-esser:** Processor reducing excessive sibilance.
- **Remote sync drift:** Increasing timing offset between recordings.
- **Integrated loudness:** Perceived program loudness over time.
- **Transcript:** Written representation of spoken content.
- **Guest release:** Permission defining use of a participant's recording/likeness.

## Purpose and theory
Voice processing depends on speaker, microphone and room. EQ can reduce rumble or improve presence; compression controls dynamic range; de-essing reduces excessive sibilance. Processing should be judged at matched loudness. Avoid forcing every voice into the same tonal profile.

## Tools, requirements and alternatives
Use a current DAW/audio editor and approved recording/transcription workflow. Record sample rate, channel layout, app/version and delivery spec. Preserve originals. Obtain consent and rights before recording, external processing or publishing.

## Procedure
1. Preserve an unprocessed reference. 2. Identify rumble, hum, hiss and room noise. 3. Apply conservative cleanup. 4. Use filtering only where needed. 5. Adjust EQ for clarity without harshness. 6. Set compression based on dynamics. 7. Apply de-essing only if sibilance is distracting. 8. Compare processed and original at matched loudness.

## Worked example
A close-mic voice has plosives and strong sibilance. A high-pass filter and gentle de-esser reduce issues without thinning the voice.

## Dry run
Aggressive denoise removes consonants. Reduce processing and accept some background noise.

## Guided practice
Create two voice chains for two different speakers and compare results.

## Independent assignment
Apply the workflow to a second speaker or episode. Submit original media, editable session, export, transcript and quality/rights notes.

## Common errors and recovery

|---|---|---|---|

## Knowledge check
1. Why is room tone useful during dialogue editing?
2. How do EQ, compression and de-essing differ?
3. Why must remote sync be checked at multiple points?
4. Why should transcripts be reviewed against audio?
5. What permission is needed for guest clips and synthetic voice?

## Answer guidance
1. It bridges edits and maintains acoustic continuity.
2. EQ changes frequency balance; compression controls dynamics; de-essing reduces sibilance.
3. Independent clocks/sample-rate behavior can cause drift.
4. ASR can mishear names, numbers, negation and speakers.
5. Guest usage rights/consent and explicit voice synthesis authorization.
