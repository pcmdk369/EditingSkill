# CT-05 Lesson 08 — Professional Advertisement Delivery and Client Handoff

- Course: CT-05 — Commercial & Advertisement Editing
- Level: Professional Practice
- Estimated time: 120–180 minutes
- Prerequisites: Previous CT-05 lessons and an approved commercial brief
- Tools/environment: Any NLE, graphics/audio tools and review log; record application/version and OS
- Version/review date: Tool-neutral; advertising rules vary by jurisdiction, product category and destination

## Learning outcomes
Package approved campaign masters and cutdowns with source project, claim approvals, licenses, captions, technical QC and a clear client handoff.

## Key definitions
- **Campaign brief:** Approved document describing objective, audience, message and deliverables.
- **Claim:** Express or implied statement about a product, service, offer or result.
- **Proof point:** Evidence supporting a claim.
- **Cutdown:** Shorter adaptation of an approved campaign master.
- **Brand guardrail:** Approved rule for identity and representation.
- **Claim ledger:** Traceable list of claims, evidence, wording and approval state.
- **Usage rights:** Contractual permission defining how, where and for how long assets may be used.

## Purpose and theory
Professional commercial delivery requires traceable approvals and precise asset identification. The final package should let the client distinguish review from approved files, know where each version may be used, and understand any restrictions. Delivery does not automatically grant rights beyond the agreed license.

## Tools, requirements and alternatives
Use a capable timeline editor, approved brand assets, a claim/rights spreadsheet and secure review storage. Verify exact application/version and destination requirements. Legal requirements vary; escalate regulated claims to qualified client/legal reviewers rather than treating this course as legal advice.

## Procedure
1. Reconcile approved brief, claims and deliverables. 2. Check music, stock, fonts, talent and location rights. 3. Confirm usage territories, media, term and exclusivity where contractually relevant. 4. Verify captions, legal copy and accessibility. 5. Export and inspect all versions. 6. Create manifest with exact file properties and approval state. 7. Package editable project and dependencies if contracted. 8. Transfer securely and confirm receipt. 9. Archive under retention policy. 10. Obtain separate portfolio permission.

## Worked example
A client receives 6-, 15- and 30-second exports with usage notes, subtitles, source project and claim approvals. Each file is labeled approved for a specific campaign and date.

## Dry run
The client asks for an editable project but the contract covers exports only. Confirm scope and quote a change request; do not assume project transfer is included.

## Guided practice
Build a mock delivery package and portfolio case study for the capstone campaign.

## Independent assignment
Complete the guided task as an independent campaign exercise. Submit editable source, review export, decision log, claim/rights records and a short reflection.

## Common errors and recovery
| Symptom | Likely cause | Diagnosis | Recovery |
|---|---|---|---|
| Submit exports, manifest, rights/claims log, project package and handoff note. | Brief or approval gap | Inspect brief, source evidence and current version | Correct and record the decision |

## Knowledge check
1. Why must a commercial edit begin with a brief and evidence review?
2. What is the difference between a feature and a substantiated claim?
3. Why is each cutdown a separate editorial deliverable?
4. What should happen to an unsupported claim?
5. What must be documented before final delivery?

## Answer guidance
1. The audience, message, restrictions and output requirements determine the edit.
2. A feature describes an attribute; a claim makes a statement that may require evidence.
3. Runtime, framing and context change; blind compression may distort meaning.
4. Hold, qualify, replace or remove it pending authorized substantiation/review.
5. Approval state, rights, claims, technical properties, captions and handoff details.

## Assessment rubric — 20 points
| Criterion | Points | Evidence |
|---|---:|---|
| Brief and audience interpretation | 4 | Written brief and message |
| Story and visual hierarchy | 4 | Storyboard/edit |
| Claim and rights discipline | 5 | Claim ledger and permissions |
| Technical and accessibility QC | 4 | Playback and export checklist |
| Versioning and handoff | 3 | Manifest and notes |

Pass: 16/20. Unsupported factual claims, rights breaches or false testimonials require correction regardless of score.

## Quality-control checklist
- [ ] Brief, audience and CTA are clear.
- [ ] Product representation is accurate.
- [ ] Every material claim has an approval/evidence state.
- [ ] Brand assets and licenses are recorded.
- [ ] Captions and legal copy are readable.
- [ ] Each version is independently reviewed.
- [ ] Handoff identifies approved files and usage restrictions.
