# CT-05 — Commercial & Advertisement Editing Assessment

**Total:** 50 points | **Suggested time:** 45–60 minutes

## Part A — Multiple choice (10 × 2 = 20)

1. The campaign brief should define: A. Audience/message/deliverables B. Only music C. Only transitions D. Export filename.
2. A product benefit claim should be: A. Invented B. Supported and approved C. Hidden in fine print D. Implied by music.
3. Visual hierarchy determines: A. File size B. What the viewer notices first C. Audio sample rate D. Frame rate.
4. A cutdown is: A. A shorter editorial adaptation B. A proxy C. A watermark D. A codec.
5. A disclaimer may be removed if runtime is short. A. Always B. Never consider readability C. Only after responsible review confirms the claim can be used without it D. Automatically.
6. Music use requires: A. A popular track B. Appropriate rights for intended use C. No documentation D. A high bitrate.
7. An unsupported “best” claim should be: A. Kept B. Substantiated, qualified or removed C. Made larger D. Repeated.
8. AI-generated product imagery should be: A. Assumed accurate B. Checked against the real product and approved facts C. Published instantly D. Used as proof.
9. Delivery permission always grants portfolio rights. A. True B. False.
10. Every cutdown should be: A. Reviewed against its own requirements B. Exported without playback C. Named “final” D. Identical except speed.

## Part B — Short answer (5 × 3 = 15)

11. Distinguish product feature, benefit and factual claim.
12. Name three items in a claim ledger.
13. Why are 6-, 15- and 30-second edits separate editorial decisions?
14. What must be checked before using a testimonial?
15. What belongs in a professional commercial handoff?

## Part C — Applied case (15)

16. A 15-second ad states “saves 30% energy” but the source study applies only to one model under specific conditions. Explain how you would verify, qualify and adapt the claim across 6-, 15- and 30-second versions. (8)
17. An AI image changes the product's visible shape and adds a feature not present in the source. Describe the appropriate response and documentation. (7)

## Answer key
1 A; 2 B; 3 B; 4 A; 5 C; 6 B; 7 B; 8 B; 9 B; 10 A.
11. Feature is an attribute; benefit is user value; claim is an express/implied assertion requiring evidence appropriate to risk. (3)
12. Claim wording, source/evidence, date, conditions, approved qualifier, reviewer/status. (3)
13. Each runtime changes story, evidence, framing and disclaimer readability. (3)
14. Truth/authorization, release scope, context and any required disclosure. (3)
15. Approved exports, manifest, rights/claims records, captions, project/dependencies if agreed and usage restrictions. (3)
16. Verify exact model and study, methodology, conditions and current validity; preserve limitation; route to authorized reviewer; ensure short version does not make claim misleading; remove if qualifier cannot be communicated. (8)
17. Reject or correct the image, preserve provenance, compare against real product, document generated tool/model and obtain approval; never present invented feature as actual. (7)

## Performance bands
45–50 exemplary; 40–44 competent; 35–39 developing; below 35 remediation. Unsupported claims or rights breaches require correction regardless of score.
