# PCMDK369™ Professional Developer & Academic Course Standard

**Owner:** PCMDK369™  
**Contact:** support.pcmdkai@gmail.com  
**Applies to:** All 30 course types, every lesson, software lab, assessment, capstone, reference guide, and every specialist module in `Agentic/EditSkill/`.  
**Status:** Mandatory repository-wide authoring standard

## 1. Purpose and governing principles

This standard makes every learning resource academically structured, technically reproducible, professionally useful, accessible, and reviewable. It governs both the learner-facing curriculum and the agent-facing specialist instructions. It supplements existing course outlines and does not replace their subject-specific learning objectives.

Every resource must:
- teach transferable principles before tool-specific procedures;
- distinguish established facts, recommendations, assumptions, and version-dependent behavior;
- define unfamiliar terminology before using it;
- include practical application and measurable evidence of learning;
- identify prerequisites, tools, alternatives, risks, and limitations;
- preserve learner/user control, source assets, privacy, licensing, and consent;
- be reviewed for technical accuracy, clarity, accessibility, and completeness.

## 2. Academic course architecture

Every one of the 30 course types must maintain this progression where relevant:

1. Foundation — concepts, vocabulary, media fundamentals, safety and setup.
2. Beginner — interface orientation, core operations and guided practice.
3. Intermediate — multi-step workflows, decision-making and integrated exercises.
4. Advanced — complex workflows, optimization, troubleshooting and constraints.
5. Expert — professional briefs, specialized production, client/stakeholder review.
6. Master — creative/technical direction, pipeline design, leadership and capstone.
7. AI & Automation — appropriate AI use, automation, evaluation, provenance and human review.
8. Professional Practice — delivery, quality assurance, collaboration, documentation, portfolio and career/studio readiness.

A course may adapt or combine stages when its discipline requires it, but must explain the mapping and preserve progression from fundamentals to independent professional competence.

## 3. Mandatory course specification

Each course outline must document:
- course ID, title, discipline, version/status and intended learner;
- course description, purpose, scope and exclusions;
- entry requirements and prerequisite knowledge;
- measurable learning outcomes using observable verbs;
- tools, hardware/software requirements and accessible alternatives;
- module/lesson map with level and estimated practice time;
- practical work, assessment strategy and grading criteria;
- capstone brief, deliverables and completion criteria;
- accessibility, rights, safety, privacy and ethical considerations;
- references, software/version notes and maintenance/review date.

## 4. Mandatory lesson specification

Every full lesson uses the shared template at `templates/academic-lesson-template.md`. At minimum include:
1. title, course ID, level, estimated time and version/date;
2. learning outcomes and prerequisites;
3. definitions and key terminology;
4. purpose, theory and underlying principles;
5. tools, requirements and alternatives;
6. numbered, reproducible procedure;
7. worked example with inputs, decisions and expected result;
8. dry run, annotated walkthrough or decision trace;
9. guided practice and independent assignment;
10. common errors, diagnosis and recovery;
11. knowledge check and answer key or instructor guidance;
12. assessment rubric with observable criteria;
13. quality-control checklist and completion evidence;
14. references and version/compatibility notes.

Do not pad lessons with generic prose. Explain the subject-specific reasoning and show how to verify the result.

## 5. Professional developer and implementation standard

For every software/tool-based course or agent module, document:
- purpose and selection criteria for each tool;
- supported platforms, versions, editions and dependencies;
- installation/setup only when needed, with official references;
- project/file structure, naming, inputs, outputs and formats;
- workflow architecture and data/media flow;
- reproducible commands or UI steps, with safe test inputs;
- expected output and validation procedure;
- error handling, troubleshooting, performance and resource limits;
- interoperability, portability, backup and rollback;
- security, privacy, licensing and operational risks.

Commands must state prerequisites, explain important flags, avoid destructive defaults, and provide a verification step. Never claim an implementation or render was executed unless evidence confirms it.

## 6. Practical labs and projects

Use `templates/professional-lab-template.md`. A lab must contain a realistic brief, prerequisites, environment, source assets, task steps, expected artifacts, validation tests, troubleshooting, submission requirements and rubric. Distinguish simulated examples from tested results. Keep source assets licensed or learner-created.

## 7. Assessment and certification-readiness

Assess knowledge, practical performance, reasoning, professional process and delivery. Use transparent criteria and point values or proficiency descriptors. Include:
- formative checks during learning;
- practical assignments with observable evidence;
- summative assessment and answer guidance where appropriate;
- project/capstone evaluation;
- remediation guidance for failed criteria.

Do not imply accreditation, official certification, employment guarantees or vendor endorsement unless independently established. This standard defines course quality; it does not itself award credentials.

## 8. Required professional quality gates

A resource is not complete until it passes:
- **Academic:** outcomes align with instruction and assessment; prerequisites are clear.
- **Technical:** steps, terms, settings, code and version claims are checked.
- **Practical:** a learner can reproduce the exercise and identify expected outputs.
- **Professional:** workflow, file organization, handoff and documentation are addressed.
- **Ethical/legal:** rights, consent, privacy, safety and AI disclosure are handled.
- **Accessibility:** structure, language, captions/alt text where applicable and readable formatting are considered.
- **Editorial:** spelling, links, headings, tables and internal references are valid.
- **Maintenance:** version-sensitive content has a review note and authoritative source.

## 9. Agent specialist module standard

Every `Agentic/EditSkill/*/SKILL.md` specialist must include:
1. module identity, scope and non-goals;
2. when to activate and required inputs;
3. core concepts and terminology;
4. professional workflow with decision points;
5. tools and capability-verification rules;
6. reproducibility, safety, rights and human approval boundaries;
7. discipline-specific quality gates;
8. troubleshooting and fallback paths;
9. evidence/reporting requirements and handoff;
10. links to relevant course types, lessons, labs and references.

Agent instructions must not invent tool access, silently execute risky actions, or replace human creative approval. For mixed-discipline work, define dependencies and handoffs.

## 10. Writing and documentation conventions

- Use clear academic/professional English; define technical terms at first use.
- Use consistent heading hierarchy, tables only when they improve comparison, and numbered steps for procedures.
- Use SI units where appropriate; identify frame rate, resolution, sample rate, bit depth, color space and loudness units precisely.
- Mark version-specific instructions and verify against authoritative documentation.
- Use relative repository links and check that targets exist.
- Separate required steps from optional enhancements.
- State assumptions and unknowns explicitly.
- Prefer inclusive, accessible examples and lawful practice assets.

## 11. Completion definition

A course or module is **authored** when its required structure and content exist. It is **reviewed** only after the quality gates are checked. It is **validated** only when reproducibility or technical checks have evidence. It is **complete** only when all required lessons, labs, assessments, capstone materials, references and reviews are finished. Never mark a course complete based solely on a populated outline.
